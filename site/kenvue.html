What Kenvue taught me about the tensions in brand management

Kenvue DFML Summit · Nov 4 – 5, 2026

Brand management, it turns out, is the art of holding two good ideas at once.

In November I flew to New Jersey for Kenvue's Developing Future Marketing Leaders (DFML) Summit: two days at headquarters with MBA students from several schools, just days after Kenvue officially became part of Kimberly-Clark. Across panels, case studies, and coffee chats, the leaders kept coming back to the same tensions.

A company that invests in leaders before it hires them

Honestly, DFML itself impressed me most! Weeks before we arrived, the team ran a case interview prep session to get us ready. At the summit, each of us got a one-on-one with an associate brand manager buddy, an honest real-talk panel with current ABMs, and mock interview prep. The whole program is built to break down barriers into brand management. They bring people in before any offers are made, so everyone leaves with sharper skills and a network across schools, whether or not they end up joining.

The proof was in the room. Two of the panelists had come through DFML themselves, and one of the leaders we heard from started as an associate brand manager intern and now leads sales for a whole business unit. They described a culture where development conversations start in your first month, new ABMs get boot camps from day one, managers map your skill gaps and push for your next rotation, and stretch assignments are normal. In the middle of a huge merger, this team still made time to invest in people who don't work there yet. That says everything about the kind of leaders Kenvue and Kimberly-Clark want to build.

Your brand vs. the retailer's category

Retailers don't care if your brand wins. They care if their category grows. So every pitch has to prove it brings in new shoppers, gets people using more, or gets them trading up. Even with AI shopping on the rise, most sales still happen in stores, so one rocky retailer relationship can sink a year.

Growth vs. cannibalization

Every new product, pack size, or bundle has to pass one filter first: is it truly incremental, or will it just pull sales from the brand's own shelf? A two-pack can look like a win and quietly shrink the single. These are the research questions I've been asking for six years, and it was so fun to see them sit at the center of brand decisions.

One global idea vs. many local markets

Global teams own the big idea, and local teams make it work in their market and channels. The hard part is language. A claim that lands in one country can fall flat in another, so the work has to resonate everywhere at once.

Moving fast vs. committing big

One leader sorted decisions into one-way and two-way doors. A digital test can fail fast and pivot. A TV campaign commits millions, so you get alignment first.

Reach vs. trust

Quick guess. Who moves an over-the-counter brand the most?

Your doctor! The panel called doctor recommendations an annuity: slow to build, and they keep paying out.

The panel on marketing to doctors flipped how I think about influence. Health brands need both creators with big audiences and the experts who publish research and teach other doctors. Patients now walk in with answers from ChatGPT, so brands also have to make sure accurate information is what AI tools learn from.

Being seen vs. making sense

One team walked us through a music festival activation for a pain-relief brand: recharge kits, a lounge with samples and phone charging, and recovery tools for tired legs. The best feedback wasn't about reach. It was festival-goers saying the brand being there actually made sense. Their lesson was to start with a business problem and a real consumer tension, not a cool execution.

The leader I'll remember most called herself a steel umbrella: she takes the pressure from above and the heat for her team's calls, so they can take smart risks. Holding the tension so your team doesn't have to might be the most important job of all. Thank you to the DFML team and every Kenvue and Kimberly-Clark leader who gave us their time and such honest answers!
