Pick a beachhead segment

PMMDrill 2.112 minIntermediate

Skills: Research & insightPositioning & strategy

The prompt

The setup. You're the PMM at a startup building an AI phone receptionist for small businesses that run on appointments. It answers calls, books and moves appointments, and sends reminders. You have the budget to go after one segment this year. The product is hypothetical; the market numbers are real.

The facts. US Census Bureau County Business Patterns, 20231:

Segment Establishments Employees Average employees per establishment Share with fewer than 5 employees
Dental offices 135,665 1,028,889 7.6 37.7%
Veterinary services 34,296 475,106 13.9 27.7%
Beauty salons 84,176 391,064 4.6 68.7%

These counts cover businesses with paid employees. They leave out the self-employed, so the true number of salons, many of them one-person businesses, is higher. The averages and shares are calculated from the Census file.

Your task:

  1. Score each segment on the questions from the lesson: size, urgency of the pain, reach, your ability to win, word of mouth, and where it leads.
  2. Pick one and defend it in three sentences.
  3. Say what you'd need to learn to change your mind.

Sample answer

Scores:

Question Dental offices Veterinary services Beauty salons
Big enough? Yes: the most establishments Smaller, but larger offices Many, but most are tiny
Urgent pain? High: a small front desk juggles calls and patients in the chair Medium: bigger staffs can cover the phone Mixed: many already use online booking apps
Reachable? Yes: dental associations, practice software, industry events Yes: veterinary associations and practice software Hard: scattered, many sole operators
Can you win? Yes, if it books into the practice's existing schedule Yes, same condition Hard: low budgets and existing booking apps
Word of mouth? Strong among dentists who know each other locally Moderate Strong, but tied to apps they already use
Where it leads Other appointment-based health care: vets, optometry, physical therapy Dental and other clinics Spas and barbershops

The pick: dental offices. They're the largest segment with paid staff, and the average office of about eight people has a front desk small enough that missed calls are a daily cost. They're reachable through associations and the software they already use. Winning there opens a clear path to other appointment-based clinics, starting with veterinary practices.

What would change my mind: interviews showing dental offices already miss few calls, or that the main practice software is about to ship its own receptionist. Either one would push the choice toward vets.

Why it works

  • It scores all three segments on the same questions instead of picking a favorite and defending it.
  • It reads past the biggest raw number: salons look large, but most are tiny and already use booking apps.
  • It names an expansion path, so the first segment leads somewhere.

What interviewers push on

  • "Why not salons? There are more of them than the Census shows." Count isn't the same as winnable. Most are one- to four-person businesses with small budgets and an app they already like.
  • "How would you check this before spending the budget?" Fifteen interviews with office managers in each segment, a look at how many calls go unanswered, and a landing page test aimed at each segment.

Practice twist

The leading dental practice-management company announces its own AI receptionist, included free for its customers. Does your pick change, and how do you position against "free and already installed"?
