Turn research into an insight

PMMDrill 2.312 minIntermediate

Skills: Research & insightWriting & storytelling

The prompt

The setup. You're the PMM for an AI coding assistant aimed at professional developers. Leadership wants a new homepage message. Here's the research you have.

The facts.

  • In Stack Overflow's 2026 Developer Survey, 65.9% of respondents said they use AI coding assistants or agents. Asked when they trust AI output, 48.0% said when they can easily verify it, 16.3% only for low-risk tasks, 12.3% not for most work tasks, and 6.6% for many tasks including important decisions1.
  • In the 2025 survey, 66% named "almost right, but not quite" answers as their top frustration with AI tools, and 45.2% said debugging AI-generated code takes more time1.
  • In a 2025 randomized trial by the research group METR, 16 experienced open-source developers worked on 246 real tasks in their own projects. Tasks where AI was allowed took 19% longer. Before the study, the developers expected AI to save 24% of their time, and afterward they still believed it had saved 20%. METR notes the sample was small and the tools were from early 20252.
  • In February 2026, METR said its newer data from late 2025, covering 57 developers, pointed to speedups instead, but called that result unreliable, partly because more developers declined to take part, since they didn't want to work without AI2.

Your task:

  1. Write three findings from the research.
  2. Group them into one theme.
  3. Write one insight in the format: who does or believes what, because why, so we should what.
  4. Recommend one change to the homepage message and one to the product or onboarding.

Sample answer

Findings:

  1. Most developers use AI coding tools, but nearly half trust the output only when they can check it, and just 6.6% trust it for important decisions.
  2. Near-misses are the main pain: answers that are almost right cost time to find and fix.
  3. Developers' sense of how much time AI saves isn't reliable. In one controlled study, they felt faster while measurably slower.

Theme: developers treat AI output as something to verify, not something to trust.

Insight: "Professional developers use AI tools widely but don't trust them on important work, because near-miss answers cost them time to check and fix, so we should compete on how easy our output is to verify, not on how fast it writes code."

Homepage message: lead with confidence, not speed. Talk about code you can check quickly, with clear explanations and tests, and drop "10x faster" claims that developers already doubt.

Product or onboarding: show the explanation and the tests with every suggestion by default, so checking takes seconds.

Why it works

  • It combines what developers say (the surveys) with what they did (the trial), and builds the insight on the gap.
  • It doesn't hang everything on one small study. The trust pattern shows up in both surveys.
  • Each part of the insight leads to a specific change.

What interviewers push on

  • "Isn't the METR study out of date?" Partly. The tools have improved and METR's newer data pointsOne value shown on a chart, such as one month's price. to speedups, though METR calls it unreliable. The insight rests mainly on trust and verification, which both surveys support.
  • "Won't 'easy to verify' sound weaker than 'faster'?" It's specific and believable, and it answers the frustration developers name most.

Practice twist

A competitor's homepage says "Ship code 10x faster with AI." Using only this research, write the two-sentence response your sales team should use when prospects bring it up.
