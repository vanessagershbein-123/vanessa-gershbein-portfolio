Map a buying group

PMMDrill 2.212 minIntermediate

Skills: Research & insight

The prompt

The facts.

  • Workday sells cloud software for HR, finance, spend management, planning and AI agents. Its annual report names the buying office for each product line: HR software sells to the office of the chief human resources officer (CHRO), financial and spend management to the office of the CFO, planning to both, and its platform and industry products to the CIO, CFO and CHRO. Direct sales teams are organized by geography, industry or customer size, with resellers and systems integrators alongside them1.
  • On November 18, 2026, Workday announced it would bring its payroll product to seven new countries, including Singapore and Spain. It also announced "autopilot" capabilities for its Payroll Agent, which checks every employee's pay before and after each payroll run, flags likely errors and prepares corrections for the payroll team to approve. Singapore is planned for full availability in January 2027, Spain is planned to follow starting in 2027, and autopilot is planned for early adopters in early 2027. More than 11,500 organizations use Workday, including more than 65% of the Fortune 5002.

Your task: You're the Workday PMM for payroll. Your target is an existing Workday HR customer, a multinational that runs payroll in Singapore and Spain through other providers today.

  1. Map the buying group: each member's role in the decision (sponsor, approver, influencer, user or possible blocker), their worry, and the proof they need.
  2. Name the person most likely to move the group, and say what you'd give them.
  3. Name the biggest risk to the deal.

Your earlier answer

Sample answer

Member Role in the decision Worry Proof they need
CHRO Sponsor Employees paid correctly everywhere; HR's reputation A similar multinational's results; how accuracy is checked
CFO Approver The cost of several payroll providers; fines for errors A business case: providers retired, errors avoided
Head of global payroll User and likely mobilizer Manual checks, changing local rules, payday errors A demo of autopilot catching errors, with the team approving every change
CIO and IT security Influencer, possible blocker Data protection, integrations, how the AI is governed Security and AI governance documentation
Country payroll leads Users Changes to their work; local rules handled correctly Local rule coverage and a migration plan
Procurement Approver of terms Contract terms with current providers Timing that matches when current contracts end

The whole table is inference from the facts above.

The mobilizer: the head of global payroll. They feel the problem every pay cycle, and they're credible to both HR and finance. Give them a one-page business case they can take to the CFO, a before-and-after of a pay cycle with autopilot, and a ready answer for IT on how the AI is controlled.

The biggest risk: timing. Spain isn't on a firm date yet and autopilot is planned for early 2027, so a promise the product can't keep would hurt trust. Sequence the deal: start with Singapore and the countries already live, and plan Spain for when it's ready.

Why it works

  • It sorts people by their role in the decision, not their title.
  • It picks the mobilizer by who can move the group, not by who's most senior or friendliest.
  • It names a real risk from the facts, the uneven country timing, instead of a generic objection.

What interviewers push on

  • "Who signs off on an AI that touches everyone's pay?" Likely IT security and legal along with the CHRO, which is why governance documentation belongs in the plan from day one.
  • "Why would they leave a provider that works?" Fewer systems and errors caught before payday. Quantify both in the business case.

Practice twist

Now the customer doesn't use Workday for HR at all. How does the buying group change, and how much bigger does the decision get?
