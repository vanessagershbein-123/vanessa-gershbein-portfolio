Get a new tier past its early adopters

PMMDrill 4.412 minAdvanced

Skills: Positioning & strategy

The prompt

The facts.

  • Box sells cloud content management to businesses. In its fiscal 2026, Box launched Enterprise Advanced, its top tier, which adds advanced AI, automated workflows and secure content management. About a year later, Box said Enterprise Advanced customers already made up 10% of its revenue1.
  • Box told investors that customers moving up to Enterprise Advanced paid an average of 30% to 40% more per seat than they did on Enterprise Plus, the tier below. Its net retention rate was 104% at the end of fiscal 2026, up from 102% a year earlier1.
  • In Rogers' adoption curve, innovators and early adopters together make up 16% of adopters, and the early majority another 34%2.

Your task: You're the PMM for Enterprise Advanced. The first year's buyers were the customers most excited about AI. Next year's growth has to come from mainstream Enterprise Plus customers who are cautious about AI and about paying 30% to 40% more.

  1. Describe who the next wave is and what's stopping them, using know, can and want.
  2. Name the proof they need and where it comes from.
  3. Plan two adoption moves for customers who already bought the tier, and one upgrade move for those who haven't.
  4. Pick the metric that tells you the tier is reaching the mainstream.

Your earlier answer

Sample answer

1. The next wave: Enterprise Plus customers in regulated or cautious industries. They know the tier exists, so "know" isn't the gap. The gaps are "want," because they aren't sure the AI is worth 30% to 40% more, and "can," because they doubt they can roll it out safely with their content and permissions.

2. The proof: results from first-year customers in similar industries, such as hours saved on a specific workflow, plus a clear account of how the AI handles permissions and sensitive content. Peer proof matters more than feature lists to this group.

3. The moves:

  • Adoption, for existing buyers: templates for the two or three workflows with the clearest payoff, offered in the product when a customer does that task, plus a usage summary for each account's business review so the buyer can show the upgrade paid off.
  • Adoption, for existing buyers: a customer advisory group of first-year champions whose results become the case studies.
  • Upgrade, for those who haven't bought: a time-limited trial of one AI workflow on the customer's own content, run with their customer success manager, ending in a business review that compares results with the price difference.

4. The metric: the share of Enterprise Advanced revenue coming from customers outside the first-year industries, alongside renewal rates for first-year buyers. If first-year customers renew and expand, the proof for the mainstream gets stronger.

Why it works

  • It uses know, can and want to find the real gap instead of defaulting to more awareness.
  • It turns early adopters' results into the proof the early majority needs.
  • It ties adoption inside existing accounts to the next wave of upgrades.

What interviewers push on

  • "Why not just discount the upgrade?" It lowers the barrier once, but it doesn't answer whether the AI is worth it, and it trains customers to wait.
  • "How do you know the first-year results are real?" Use the customers' own usage data and agree on the measure with them, then let them tell the story.

Practice twist

First-year renewals come in and a third of Enterprise Advanced customers want to move back down to Enterprise Plus. What do you investigate first, and what does it change about the plan for the next wave?
