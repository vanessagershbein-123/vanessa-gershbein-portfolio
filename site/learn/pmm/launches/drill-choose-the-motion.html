Choose the motion

PMMDrill 4.210 minIntermediate

Skills: Positioning & strategy

The prompt

The facts.

  • Cloudflare's website and network plans run from Free ($0) to Pro ($25 a month, or $20 billed annually) and Business ($250 a month, or $200 annually), all bought online, plus custom Contract plans, billed annually and sold through sales1.
  • At the end of 2025, Cloudflare had about 332,000 paying customers in more than 190 countries, and 4,298 large customers paying more than $100,000 a year, up from 2,756 two years earlier. Self-serve customers sign up on the website or through hosting partners and pay by card without talking to sales. Enterprise customers are sold through inside and field sales teams and channel partners, and a substantial majority of 2025 revenue came from those contracted customers2.
  • On July 1, 2025, Cloudflare launched Pay per crawl, which lets website owners allow, charge or block each AI crawler that visits their site, at a flat price per request, with Cloudflare collecting the payments3.

Your task: You're the PMM for Pay per crawl, before launch. Decide how it should reach customers first: self-serve to every plan, sales-led to large customers, or partner-led through hosting partners. Defend your choice, and say what would make you add the other motions later.

Your earlier answer

Sample answer

Lead with sales. The sites AI companies most want to crawl, such as large publishers and media companies, are the ones with valuable content, and many of them are likely among Cloudflare's large enterprise customers. Charging AI crawlers is new, so the first customers need help setting prices and policies, and their legal teams will want a say. That's a conversation for an account team, not a sign-up page.

Add self-serve later. Once a few large sites show what prices work and how crawlers respond, sensible defaults can go to the self-serve plans, where most of Cloudflare's customers are. Hosting partners come after that, for the long tail of small sites.

What Cloudflare actually did: it launched Pay per crawl as a private beta with two ways in: a public sign-up form for interested publishers and crawlers, and the account executive for existing Enterprise customers3.

Why it works

  • It starts with the customers for whom the product is worth the most, and with the motion that can handle a new, judgment-heavy decision.
  • It uses the company's real mix: most customers self-serve, but most revenue comes through sales.
  • It names the trigger for adding motions: proven prices and defaults.

What interviewers push on

  • "Why not open it to all paying customers at once for maximum reach?" Small sites get few valuable crawler requests, and a confusing new pricing decision could turn into support tickets instead of revenue.
  • "What would make you lead with self-serve instead?" If the product could set good prices automatically and most of the value sat with small sites.

Practice twist

Now the product is a usage-priced developer tool with a free tier, aimed at individual engineers. Which motion leads, and when would sales get involved?
