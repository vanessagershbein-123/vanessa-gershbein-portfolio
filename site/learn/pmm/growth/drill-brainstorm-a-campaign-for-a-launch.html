Brainstorm a campaign for a launch

PMMDrill 7.115 minIntermediate

Skills: Writing & storytelling

The prompt

The facts.

  • On September 30, 2025, OpenAI released Sora 2, its most advanced video-generation model, along with a new iOS app also called Sora, aimed at bringing AI video creation to everyday users. The app let people create, remix and "cameo" in AI-generated videos. Real people couldn't be generated unless they'd submitted a verified cameo of themselves. It launched invite-only in the US and Canada, with a Pro version, web access and a developer API to follow1.

Your task: You're the PMM for the Sora app at launch. Plan the first 90 days as a campaign: the brief (audience, one message, one goal), the idea, the channels and how you'll measure success. Be specific about the number that would tell you, by week four, whether the launch is really working.

Your earlier answer

Sample plan

  • Audience: people who already make and share short videos, starting with groups of friends rather than solo users.
  • One message: "Put yourself and your friends in any scene."
  • The idea: make the cameo itself the campaign. Every new user records a cameo and gets invites to share, so each invite comes with a reason to make something together.
  • Channels: seed invites with creators whose audiences love remixing, let finished videos travel to other social platforms with a clear Sora mark, and use the waitlist to build demand.
  • Measure: not downloads. Watch the share of each week's new users who come back in week four, the share who make a video with a friend's cameo, and videos shared outside the app.

What happened

The launch itself was a hit. The app passed 100,000 installs on day one despite being invite-only, hit No. 1 on the US App Store and reached 1 million downloads faster than ChatGPT had. Then it faded. According to Appfigures data, downloads fell 32% month over month in December, a month when apps usually do well, and another 45% in January. By then the app had about 9.6 million downloads and $1.4 million in consumer spending in total2.

On March 24, 2026, six months after launch, OpenAI said it was shutting Sora down. CNBC reported that the initial excitement had faded and that OpenAI was cutting costs ahead of a possible IPO3.

What you can learn from it

  • A launch spike isn't growth. The invite mechanic and the novelty drove acquisition. Falling downloads and spending in the months after suggest that keeping users was the harder problem.
  • Pick a week-four number before launch. Downloads and chart rankings said the launch worked. A retention goal would have shown whether people were actually sticking around.
  • Cost belongs in the brief. AI video is expensive to generate, so every active user costs real money. The economics of keeping users matter as much as attracting them.

What interviewers push on

  • "Wasn't the invite system a smart loop?" It was a strong acquisition loop. A loop also needs people to stay long enough to invite the next round.
  • "What would you have changed?" Tie the launch to repeat use, like weekly prompts to make something with friends, and judge success by week-four retention, not chart position.

Practice twist

You're launching an AI photo-editing feature inside an app people already use daily. How does your campaign change when you don't need downloads at all?
