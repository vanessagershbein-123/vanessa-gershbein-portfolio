Restart growth for a stalled product

PMMDrill 7.215 minIntermediate

Skills: Data & chartsPositioning & strategy

The prompt

The facts.

  • Pinterest ended 2021 with 431 million monthly active users, down 6% from a year earlier. In the US, users fell 12%, from 98 million to 86 million. Pinterest blamed the end of the pandemic boom, lower search traffic after a Google algorithm change, and growing competition for people's time from video-centric apps. Revenue still grew 52% for the year, to $2.58 billion1.
  • By mid-2022, users had leveled off at 433 million, still down 5% from a year earlier. Pinterest had struggled to hold on to the users it gained early in the pandemic. Co-founder Ben Silbermann stepped down as CEO in June and was replaced by Bill Ready, whose experience at Google and PayPal was expected to help Pinterest grow its online shopping business. The activist investor Elliott Management said it had become Pinterest's largest investor and backed Ready2.

Your task: It's mid-2022 and you're a PMM at Pinterest. Diagnose which stage of growth stalled, then plan how to restart it. Say which users you'd focus on, what would bring them back or keep them, and which number you'd watch.

Your earlier answer

Sample answer

  • Diagnosis: this is mostly a retention and engagement problem, plus an acquisition hit from search. Pandemic users came for a specific season of life and didn't find a reason to stay, while video apps competed for the same time.
  • Focus: younger users, where video apps are winning, and the US, where the drop was steepest.
  • What brings them back: make Pinterest the place you go to act on an idea, not just save it. If a saved outfit or room idea leads straight to products you can buy, there's a reason to return every time you're planning something.
  • Number to watch: the share of users who return in a given month, broken out by age group, plus how many sessions end with someone clicking through to a product.

What Pinterest actually did

Pinterest leaned into shopping. In April 2023 it signed a multiyear deal making Amazon its first partner for third-party ads, bringing more brands and products to the platform at a time when its image pinboard had started to feel dated next to TikTok and Reels3. In October 2025 it launched Pinterest Assistant, an AI shopping helper that gives recommendations based on your taste4.

Global users grew again. Pinterest ended 2025 with 619 million monthly active users worldwide, and Gen Z made up more than half of its user base5.

What you can learn from it

  • Name the stage before the fix. Pinterest named two causes: fewer people returning after the pandemic, and less traffic from Google search. A reason to come back addresses the first, while search traffic needs its own plan.
  • Fit the product to the job. Pinterest leaned on what people already used it for, planning things, and made the next step easier.
  • Watch who's growing. By 2025, Gen Z made up more than half of Pinterest's users, so younger users ended up central to the comeback, even though the numbers can't say which moves brought them in.
  • Be careful about credit. Many moves happened at once, so no single one can claim the turnaround.

What interviewers push on

  • "Why not just chase video like everyone else?" Pinterest tried creator video, but matching a rival's format puts you on their ground. Its edge was intent: people arrive planning to do something.
  • "How would you know the plan was working before user numbers moved?" Watch return visits and click-throughs in the target group month by month.

Practice twist

A meal-kit company lost a third of its subscribers after the pandemic ended. What stage stalled, and what's the first thing you'd test?
