Pick the one growth lever to pull

PMMDrill 7.312 minIntermediate

Skills: Data & chartsBusiness math

The prompt

The facts.

  • In the third quarter of 2026, Tinder's monthly active users were down 5.5% from a year earlier, an improvement from the quarter before. Among women, the decline narrowed from 8% to 6%. Daily active users were down just 1%, and turned to year-over-year growth in September. Sparks, which counts users in multi-message conversations, rose 1%. Paying users fell 4% to 8.9 million, while revenue per payer rose 2% to $18.02, leaving Tinder's direct revenue down 2% at $480 million. Match Group said it was spending more on marketing at Tinder and Hinge, and that its focus was "reconsideration," particularly among younger Gen Z users. Its sister app Hinge, meanwhile, grew paying users 23% to 2.3 million1.

Your task: You run growth marketing for Tinder next year and can fund one lever: acquisition, activation, retention, referral or revenue. Pick one, say who it targets and defend why it beats the others.

Your earlier answer

Sample answer

Pull acquisition, aimed at bringing lapsed and new Gen Z users back to take another look.

  • Why not revenue: revenue per payer is already rising. Pushing harder on price with a shrinking user base can speed the decline.
  • Why not retention or activation alone: daily users and Sparks are improving, so the product changes for existing users seem to be landing. The constraint is the number of people willing to try Tinder at all.
  • Why acquisition: payers come from users. Paying users fell 4% while users fell 5.5%, so the pool is the problem, especially among the young users the company says it has the most ground to recover with.
  • How: change how Gen Z sees the app, not just how many ads they see. Lead with lower-pressure, social ways to meet, and give users a reason to bring a friend, which turns acquisition into a referral loop.
  • Number to watch: new and returning Gen Z users each month, and how many of them reach a real conversation in their first week.

What Match Group said it's doing

Match Group described its next step as driving broader reconsideration, giving more people a reason to see Tinder differently and take another look. For the fourth quarter, it said it was doubling down on a "Better with Friends" roadmap, with features like Double Date and Events designed to give Gen Z more social, lower-pressure ways to meet, often with friends and in real life1.

Why this choice holds up

  • It follows the numbers. Engagement is improving and monetization is fine, so the shrinking top of the funnel is what's left.
  • It pairs a lever with a loop. Meeting with friends gives every user a reason to recruit the next one.
  • Hinge shows people still pay for a dating app that feels different. Its paying users grew 23% while Tinder's fell.

What interviewers push on

  • "Isn't 'reconsideration' just a brand campaign?" Partly. The product has to change too, or the campaign brings people back to the same experience that made them leave.
  • "Why not invest in Hinge instead?" It's a portfolio question worth raising, but the task was Tinder's growth, and Tinder still has far more paying users.

Practice twist

Six months later, Gen Z users are returning, but few of them pay. Which lever do you pull now?
