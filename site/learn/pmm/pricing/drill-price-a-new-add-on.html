Price a new add-on

PMMDrill 5.112 minIntermediate

Skills: Pricing & promotionBusiness math

The prompt

The facts.

  • Airtable's self-serve plans, billed annually, are Free, Team at $20 per seat a month and Business at $45 per seat a month. Enterprise Scale is sold through sales1.
  • Airtable AI gives customers access to models from developers including OpenAI, Google, Anthropic, Meta and IBM. Airtable has sold it to Team and Business customers as a per-seat add-on2.
  • Every AI request costs money to run, and use inside a workspace tends to be uneven: a few people may do most of the AI work while others barely touch it.

Your task: Put yourself in spring 2025, when Airtable still sold AI as a per-seat add-on. You're the PMM for Airtable AI. Decide how AI should be priced from here: keep the per-seat add-on, include unlimited AI in every plan, or try something else. Say what each plan includes, what extra use costs and how you'd move current add-on customers over.

Your earlier answer

Sample answer

Include a monthly allowance in every plan, and sell extra by usage. Per seat is the wrong meter. A customer pays for every seat even if two people do all the AI work, so many customers skip it. Unlimited AI has the opposite problem, because heavy users cost real money.

  • Plans: give every plan a pool of AI credits that grows with the plan, so Business gets more than Team. Share the pool across the workspace so heavy users can draw on it.
  • Extra use: sell credit packs at one simple rate.
  • Free: a small allowance that shows the value, with more only after upgrading.
  • Current customers: refund the unused part of the add-on, so nobody pays twice during the switch.

What Airtable actually did

Starting June 24, 2025, Airtable stopped charging Team and Business customers for AI per seat, and refunded unused add-on time as billing credits. Every plan now includes monthly AI credits: 500 per editor on Free, 15,000 per paid user on Team, 20,000 on Business and 25,000 on Enterprise Scale. Credits are pooled across the workspace or organization. Extra credits come in packs from 10,000 to 200,000 a month at $2 per 1,000, and Free customers have to upgrade to Team to buy more2.

Why it works

  • The meter matches the cost. Credits track actual AI use, not headcount.
  • Pooling fits how teams work. One power user and ten light users can share the same allowance.
  • AI makes the higher plans better. The included allowance grows by plan instead of being a separate purchase.
  • Free stays a funnel. Free customers get a taste, and buying more requires a paid plan.

What interviewers push on

  • "Won't usage pricing make bills unpredictable?" That's why there's a generous included pool and fixed-size packs, not open-ended metering.
  • "Why not keep the add-on and cut its price?" A cheaper per-seat price still charges for people who never use AI.
  • "How would you set the included amounts?" Look at how much AI a typical workspace uses now, and size the pools so most customers never need a pack.

Practice twist

Now the AI feature does one specific job, like an agent that answers customer questions on its own. Would you still sell credits, or charge for each question it resolves?
