Plan a price increase

PMMDrill 5.312 minIntermediate

Skills: Pricing & promotionWriting & storytelling

The prompt

The facts.

  • Going into October 2025, Xbox Game Pass had three main tiers: Core at $10 a month, Standard at $15 and Ultimate at $20, after Ultimate went up from $17 in July 2024. PC Game Pass cost $12. Microsoft was about to add more to Ultimate: Ubisoft+ (about $16 a month on its own), Fortnite Crew from November (usually about $12 a month), at least 75 day-one games a year and better cloud streaming. Sony's PlayStation Plus Premium cost $160 a year on an annual plan1.
  • New partner content costs money to license, so the Ultimate price is going up.

Your task: You're the PMM for Game Pass in September 2025. Plan the Ultimate price increase: how big, how fast, who's protected, what you say and what you'll watch.

Your earlier answer

Sample answer

Raise it, but not all at once.

  • How much: move Ultimate to around $25 now, with room to go higher once the new content proves itself. A one-step jump of 50% invites every subscriber to rethink the purchase.
  • Who's protected: add a discounted annual or quarterly option, and give current members a few months at their old price.
  • Where they land: keep Standard at $15, so price-sensitive players step down instead of canceling.
  • What you say: lead with the new value in dollars. Ubisoft+ and Fortnite Crew alone cost about $28 a month bought separately.
  • What you watch: cancellations, downgrades and annual renewals, weekly for the first two months.

What Xbox actually did

On October 1, 2025, Microsoft raised Ultimate to $30 a month, 50% more, with no annual or quarterly option. That was a 76% rise in 15 months, from $17. Core and Standard were renamed Essential and Premium at unchanged prices, and PC Game Pass went from $12 to $16.50. Ultimate got the new content, and the Game Pass cancellation page appeared to be overloaded that day1.

Less than seven months later, on April 21, 2026, Xbox cut Ultimate to $22.99 and PC Game Pass to $13.99. It also said future Call of Duty games would no longer join those plans at launch, arriving about a year later instead. Xbox said the change responded to a lot of feedback2.

What you can learn from it

  • One big jump gave subscribers a reason to cancel. That's the reaction a gradual approach is meant to avoid.
  • Offering no annual or quarterly option left loyal members no cushion to soften the increase.
  • The reversal came with a trade. The price fell, but day-one Call of Duty went away. Cutting a price usually means cutting value too.
  • Real value can still get drowned out. The new content was substantial, but the size of the jump became the story.

What interviewers push on

  • "Isn't $30 still a good deal for all that content?" Maybe on paper, but customers judge an increase against what they paid last month, not against a bundle they never bought.
  • "Why not raise it and accept the churnThe share of customers, or of revenue, lost in a period.?" Lost subscribers are expensive to win back, and the April 2026 cut suggests the trade didn't pay off.

Practice twist

You're raising the price of a B2BBusiness-to-business: selling to companies rather than to consumers. tool by 15% for existing customers on annual contracts. How does the plan change when customers renew once a year and a procurement team reads every notice?
