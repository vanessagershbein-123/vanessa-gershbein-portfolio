Parity or difference? AI assistants at work

PMMDrill 3.210 minIntermediate

Skills: Positioning & strategy

The prompt

The facts.

  • Notion: Notion AI launched to everyone in February 2023 as an add-on at $10 per member a month, less with annual billing1.
  • Microsoft: Microsoft 365 Copilot went on sale to large companies on November 1, 2023, at $30 per user a month2. In January 2025, Microsoft added Copilot to its Personal and Family plans and raised the US price by $3 a month2.
  • Google: in January 2025, Google stopped selling its Gemini add-ons and included its AI features in Workspace Business and Enterprise plans, with new pricing3.
  • Atlassian: its Rovo AI assistant started rolling out to Premium and Enterprise plans at no extra cost in April 20254.

Your task: It's April 2025, and you're the PMM for Notion AI. Your VP asks whether selling AI as an add-on still makes sense.

  1. Is "has an AI assistant" still a point of difference for Notion?
  2. Build a parity-and-difference table against Google Workspace, for a team choosing where its docs and wiki should live.
  3. Recommend what Notion should do with the add-on.

Your earlier answer

Sample answer

1. No. Once Google and Atlassian put AI into their plans, basic AI assistance became table stakes for work software. Charging extra for it makes Notion look more expensive, not better.

2. The table:

Attribute Notion Google Workspace Call
AI that drafts and summarizes in docs Yes, as a paid add-on Included in Business and Enterprise plans Parity, and Notion charges extra for it
Docs, wiki and project tracking in one place Yes Spread across separate apps Difference, for teams that run projects inside their docs
Email and calendar Newer apps of its own Gmail and Google Calendar, which many teams already live in Google's strength. Don't fight on it

3. The call: fold AI into the main plans, and move the message from "has AI" to what the AI does with a team's own workspace, such as finding answers across its docs and projects.

What Notion actually did: in May 2025, it launched a new Business plan with its newest AI features, such as search across connected tools, built in1. That put AI into a higher tier rather than giving it to everyone, so AI became a reason to upgrade.

Why it works

  • It spots the moment a difference turns into parity: when the main rivals build it into their base plans.
  • It moves the fight to a frame Notion can win (docs, wiki and projects together) and away from Google's home ground.
  • It checks the answer against what the company did.

What interviewers push on

  • "Notion moved AI into a pricier plan instead of giving it to everyone. Is that the same thing?" Not quite. It's a packaging choice that turns AI into a reason to upgrade.
  • "Doesn't bundling AI cut revenue?" Maybe at first. The case is that a separate charge now reads as a fee for table stakes, and the plan price can carry the cost.
  • "What's the next difference?" Something the AI can do that rivals' can't, because of what Notion already knows about a team's work.

Practice twist

Flip it. You're the PMM for Google Workspace, and Notion now includes AI too. What becomes your difference?
