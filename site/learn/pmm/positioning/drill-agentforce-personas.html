One AI agent, two buyers, one test

PMMDrill 3.412 minAdvanced

Skills: Writing & storytellingResearch & insight

The prompt

The facts. Salesforce made Agentforce generally available on October 29, 2024. At launch, pricing started at $2 per conversation. It includes a low-code Agent Builder and the Einstein Trust Layer, with an audit trail. In Salesforce's announcement, the publisher Wiley reported more than a 40% increase in case resolution1.

Your task: It's late 2024, right after launch.

  1. Write the launch message for a VP of Customer Service in the awareness stage.
  2. Rewrite it for a CIO in the decision stage who is worried about trust and control.
  3. Design a quick test that tells you whether each message works for its audience.

Your earlier answer

Sample answer

VP of Customer Service (awareness): "Your customers wait in a queue while your team answers the same questions over and over. AI agents can now resolve routine cases any time of day, so your people can focus on the ones that need a human."

CIO (decision): "Agentforce runs inside the Salesforce platform you already govern. The Einstein Trust Layer and an audit trail show what every agent did and why. Pricing starts at $2 per conversation, so cost is tied to use, and you can start with one use case. Wiley, an early customer, reported more than a 40% increase in case resolution."

Same core value: customers get answers faster, at lower cost. The VP hears about the queue and the team. The CIO hears about control, audits and cost tied to use, because a CIO answers for what runs on company systems.

The test: a qualitative message test with about 12 people from each audience (VPs of service, CIOs). Ask each group what the message says, who it's for, whether it's relevant to them, and what would make them doubt it. Add a five-second test on the launch page hero to see whether people catch "AI agents for customer service" at a glance.

Why it works

  • The core value stays the same while the framing, stage and proof change.
  • Proof shows up at the decision stage, where the buyer is comparing vendors, so the CIO gets governance, pricing and a customer result while the awareness message for the VP stays educational.
  • A qualitative test fits because the page hasn't launched yet and the panel needs specific roles, like CIOs, that a site-wide A/B test can't target.

What interviewers push on

  • "One customer's 40% isn't proof for everyone. How do you use it?" Name it as one customer's result, and gather more cases quickly.
  • "What would make the CIO say no?" Data access and accuracy risks, so the message has to answer those directly.

Practice twist

Add a third persona: the frontline support agent who worries an AI agent will replace them. What do they need to hear, and who should say it?
