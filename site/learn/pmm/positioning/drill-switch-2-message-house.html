A message house for the Switch 2 upgrade

PMMDrill 3.310 minIntermediate

Skills: Writing & storytellingPositioning & strategy

The prompt

The facts. Nintendo Switch 2 launched June 5, 2025, at $449.99. It has a 7.9-inch 1080p screen, up to 4K output when docked, 256GB of storage, and it plays compatible original Switch games, though Nintendo says some aren't fully supported. GameChat, a built-in chat feature, could be used without a Nintendo Switch Online membership until the end of March 2026. The new Joy-Con 2 controllers can work like a mouse1. The screen supports variable refresh rates up to 120Hz1.

Your task: It's spring 2025, before launch. Your audience is people who already own an original Switch and are deciding whether to upgrade. Write:

  1. A positioning statement (For / who are dissatisfied with / our product is a / that provides / unlike / we have assembled)
  2. A message house with a roof line and three pillars, each with one proof point

Your earlier answer

Sample answer

Statement: For Switch owners who love their library but are dissatisfied with how it looks and plays on the original hardware, Nintendo Switch 2 is the next Switch, and it plays the compatible games they already own. Unlike the Switch they have now, it adds a bigger, sharper 7.9-inch screen, 4K on the TV and built-in chat.

Roof: "Everything you love about Switch, bigger and better."

Keep your library See it better Play together
Message Your games come with you A bigger, sharper screen, and 4K on the TV Chat and play with friends built in
Proof Plays compatible original Switch games 7.9-inch 1080p screen at up to 120Hz; up to 4K docked GameChat (Switch Online membership required after March 2026)

Why it works

  • The real alternative for this audience is keeping the Switch they have, so the first pillar removes the biggest reason not to upgrade: losing their games.
  • Each pillar has a concrete proof point from the facts.
  • It's honest about the membership requirement instead of hiding it.

What interviewers push on

  • "How do you handle the $449.99 price?" Lead with the library pillar, so the price reads as an upgrade to what they already own.
  • "Why not lead with the mouse controls?" They're new, but it's unclear yet whether upgraders care. Test them before making them a pillar.

Practice twist

Same product, new audience: a family that has never owned a Switch. What changes in the roof and pillars?
