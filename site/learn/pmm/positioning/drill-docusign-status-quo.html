Position contract software against doing nothing

PMMDrill 3.110 minIntermediate

Skills: Positioning & strategy

The prompt

The facts.

  • In April 2024, Docusign launched Intelligent Agreement Management (IAM) and called it a new software category. Its pitch: most companies are stuck in an "agreement trap," with agreements stuck in static files, disconnected from the company's systems of record. It cited an upcoming study with Deloitte estimating that each business function loses about 25,000 hours a year developing agreements1.
  • IAM includes Navigator, which uses AI to turn stored contracts into searchable data, and Maestro, for building agreement workflows without code1.
  • By January 2026, Docusign had more than 1.8 million customers. More than 25,000 of them used IAM, which made up 10.8% of annual recurring revenueAnnual recurring revenue: the yearly value of subscription contracts.2.
  • Matthew Dixon and Ted McKenna analyzed more than 2.5 million recorded sales calls and found that 40% to 60% of deals are lost to buyers who intend to buy but never act3.

Your task: You're the PMM for Docusign IAM, selling to a company that already signs its contracts in Docusign.

  1. List the real alternatives for that buyer. Include doing nothing.
  2. Pick the one you lose to most, and write a two-sentence positioning against it.
  3. Name one thing you'd give sales for a buying group that agrees with the problem but stalls.

Your earlier answer

Sample answer

The alternatives:

  • Keep signing in Docusign and storing contracts in shared drives, as they do today
  • A contract management tool from another vendor
  • Build workflows in tools they already own, such as their CRM
  • Do nothing, because no single team owns the problem

The one that matters most: the way they work today. Contracts already get signed, and the cost of the agreement trap is spread across legal, sales and finance, so no one feels it enough to push for a change. That's the indecision Dixon and McKenna describe.

Positioning: "Your contracts already get signed in Docusign, then disappear into folders no system can read. IAM turns them into data your sales, legal and finance teams can search and act on, without changing how anyone signs."

For a stalled group: a small first step that limits the risk of a bad call, such as starting with one team's contracts, plus a one-page business case each function can check against its own numbers.

Why it works

  • It treats the current process, not another vendor, as the main competitor.
  • It builds on what the buyer already trusts (e-signature) instead of asking them to replace anything.
  • It plans for the stall, because a buying group can agree on the problem and still not act.

What interviewers push on

  • "Why not lead with the Deloitte numbers?" Docusign helped run that study, so a skeptical buyer will discount it. The buyer's own numbers persuade better.
  • "Who in the buying group cares most?" Legal owns the contracts, but sales and finance feel the delays, so the message needs a line for each.

Practice twist

Now the buyer has never used Docusign and still signs on paper. How does your list of alternatives change, and which one matters most?
