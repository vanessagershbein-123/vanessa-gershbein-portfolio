Consumer (B2C) product marketing

PMMLesson 1.310 min

Goal: Explain how consumer PMM differs from B2B, and what a consumer PMM owns, measures and tests.

Skills: Interviews & careerPositioning & strategy

Someone on a bus is thumbing through an app store page. They glance at the screenshots, skim a review or two and decide whether to download before the next stop. Nobody from your company is there to explain anything.

That's the world of consumer product marketing. Instead of a buying group that takes months, you're reaching millions of people who each decide alone, often in a few seconds on an app store page or an upgrade screen. There's usually no sales team to explain the product, so the product itself, its store page and the messages inside the app have to do that work. The thinking carries over from B2BBusiness-to-business: selling to companies rather than to consumers.. The tools and the pace don't.

How one person decides

Even a fast decision has steps. OpenStax's Principles of Marketing breaks the consumer decision into five stages: need recognition, information search, evaluation of alternatives, the purchase decision and post-purchase evaluation1. What changes from business buying is the crowd and the clock. There are far more buyers, each of whom decides alone or with a household, and decisions take less time1.

In a consumer app, every stage happens somewhere specific, and that's where your work shows up:

Stage Where it happens in a consumer app What the PMM shapes
Need recognition A friend's post, an ad, a moment of frustration Campaigns with brand and social teams, and the story people share
Information search The app store page, reviews, the website Store screenshots and copy, and what press and reviewers hear at launch
Evaluation of alternatives A free tier or a trial Onboarding that gets people to a first win fast
Purchase decision The upgrade screen Which plan to show, what it includes and how it's worded
Post-purchase evaluation The first weeks as a paying user Lifecycle emails and in-app messages that keep people using what they paid for

Don't skim past the last row. Many consumer subscriptions can be canceled any month, so your customer makes the purchase decision again every billing cycle, and annual plans move that decision to renewal time.

What's on a consumer PMM's plate

Read a real job description and the difference jumps out. Duolingo's posting for a Duolingo Math PMM is a good snapshot: plans for acquisition, engagement and retention, launch go-to-marketGo-to-market: the plan for how a product reaches and wins customers, covering the target, message, pricing, channels and launch., consumer research, and campaigns across in-app messages, app store pages and email2. Notice what's missing: no pitch deck, no battlecardA short internal guide that helps sales handle a specific competitor: where you win, where you lose and how to answer objections.. When your audience is millions of individuals, you work through channels that reach them at scale, and your closest partners are the growth, lifecycle, brand and PR teams.

Scale comes with a perk: testing gets cheap. Apple's product page optimization lets an app test up to three alternate versions of its App Store page against the original, changing the icon, screenshots or preview videos, for up to 90 days. It then reports which version converts better and how confident the result is3. A B2B PMM rarely gets that clean a read on a message. A consumer PMM can get one in weeks.

The numbers you'll be judged on

Downloads and sign-ups feel like wins, but they only show the top of the funnel. A consumer PMM is judged on what happens after them, using standard consumer subscription metrics like these:

Metric What it tells you
Monthly active users (MAU) How many people use the product at all
Daily active users (DAU) How many have made it a habit
Conversion to paid What share of users decide it's worth paying for
Churn How many paying users leave each month
Retention by cohort Whether people who joined in a given month are still around later

Worked example: Duolingo keeps the lessons free

Duolingo gives its lessons away on purpose. Free learners see an ad after each lesson, and paid tiers remove the ads and add features. In 2025, paid subscribers were 9.2% of average monthly users, and the company says its growth comes mainly from word of mouth and brand buzz, with selective paid marketing4.

Its Q3 2026 shareholder letter brought a change to the lineup. Along with the numbers below, it said Duolingo would stop selling its AI tier, Max, and fold its Video Call feature into the Super plan4. Max had launched in March 2023 as a tier above Super, built around AI features such as Explain My Answer and Roleplay, with Video Call added later5.

Q3 2026 Duolingo
Monthly active users 153.1 million
Daily active users 64.9 million
Paid subscribers 13.1 million

Divide DAU by MAU and you get a rough habit score: about 42% that quarter. If you were the PMM, three things would follow:

  • The free tier is the acquisition engine. Messages to free learners aim at habit and sharing, because a learner who keeps a streak and tells a friend brings in more users than an ad does.
  • The upgrade happens in a few moments. That's where the PMM has the most say: the upgrade screen, trial offers and the prompts that appear when a free learner wants something the free tier doesn't have.
  • Tiers need clear jobs. An AI feature that once justified a premium tier is moving into the main paid plan. You can watch it turn into table stakes, right on the price list.

B2B and consumer, side by side

B2B, sales-led Consumer
Who decides A buying group One person, or a household
How long it takes Weeks to months Seconds to days
What the message leans on Business value, risk and proof Everyday benefit, habit, what friends use
Where the message lives Sales conversations, decks, demos, the website The store page, the product, social and email
Main PMM outputs Enablement, persona messaging, customer proof Lifecycle and in-app messages, store pages, campaigns, tests
Typical metrics Pipeline, win rate, deal size Active users, conversion to paid, churn

Real companies are messier than any table. A consumer app that adds a team plan picks up B2B work, and a B2B tool with a free plan picks up consumer-style work.

Common traps

  • Assuming consumer PMM means advertising. Postings like Duolingo's lean on lifecycle, onboarding and in-app messages, often inside the product itself.
  • Counting downloads as success. A download that never becomes a habit doesn't pay. Watch active use and conversion instead.
  • Sending one message to everyone. New, free, paying and lapsed users need different things from you.

In an interview

Consumer cases often ask how you'd grow usage or get free users to pay. It's tempting to jump straight to a clever campaign. Hold off. First say which stage of the funnel you're fixing and which metric should move, so the interviewer can see you're solving the right problem.

Self-check: A meditation app's downloads doubled after a viral video, but paid subscribers barely moved. Where do you look first?

Between the download and the first real session. If new users don't finish a first meditation, they never reach the upgrade screen, so check activation before touching price or the paywall. Then compare how this group converts against earlier users, since people who arrived from a viral video may want something different.

Check your understanding

3 questions. Pick an answer to see if you're right.

  1. What changes most for a PMM moving from sales-led B2B to a consumer app?

    Show answer

    There's no sales team, so the store page, the product and in-app messages have to carry the message. Consumers decide alone and at scale, so the self-serve touchpoints do the work a seller does in B2B.

  2. You want to know which App Store screenshots get more iPhone users to download your app. What gives you the most trustworthy answer?

    Show answer

    A product page optimization test that shows alternate screenshots to real App Store visitors. Real visitors deciding whether to download show you what converts. Subscribers, coworkers and friends give you opinions, and they already know the app.

  3. About 9% of Duolingo's monthly users pay. What does the free tier do for the business?

    Show answer

    It brings in learners, largely through word of mouth, and a share of them later upgrade. Duolingo says growth comes mainly from word of mouth and brand buzz, and its paid tiers sit on top of a free product that shows ads.
