One feature, two buyers: HubSpot

PMMDrill 1.210 minIntermediate

Skills: Positioning & strategy

The prompt

The facts.

  • Each HubSpot product line, which it calls a Hub, comes in free and paid tiers, so customers can get value before they pay. HubSpot had 288,706 customers in more than 135 countries at the end of 2025, and says it targets mid-market companies with 2 to 2,000 employees1.
  • Its sales reps work by phone, email and web meetings, and some upgrades inside the product happen with little rep involvement. Larger customers get a dedicated customer success manager, while others are served mainly through digital channels. Partners who resell and refer, and the customers they bring, account for about 25% of customers and about 49% of revenue1.
  • In fall 2025, HubSpot announced more than 200 new products and features at once, including AI agents such as a Prospecting Agent2.

Your task: You're a HubSpot PMM launching one new paid AI feature. It has to reach two customers: a 12-person agency on HubSpot's free tools, and a 1,500-person company on a top-tier plan with a customer success manager.

  1. For each customer, say who decides, how they'd buy, and where your message should show up.
  2. List what you'd build for each.
  3. Pick one metric for each that tells you the launch worked.

Your earlier answer

Sample answer

12-person agency, free tools 1,500-person company, top tier
Who decides The owner, often alone A buying group: the marketing lead, sales operations, IT and finance
How they buy Inside the product, by upgrading Through their customer success manager, a sales rep or a partner
Where the message lives An in-product prompt at the moment they need it, email, the pricing page An account review, a demo, a business case
What the PMM builds An in-app announcement, a short video, upgrade page copy A pitch deck, demo script, one-page business case, and training for reps, success managers and partners
Metric Upgrades from free to the paid tier that includes the feature Pipeline and win rate on deals that include it

All of the table is inference from the facts above.

Why it works

  • It treats one feature as two launches, because the two customers buy in completely different ways.
  • It doesn't forget partners, who bring in about half of HubSpot's revenue, so they need enablement too.
  • It ties each metric to the motion: upgrades for self-serve, pipeline and win rate for sales-led.

What interviewers push on

  • "Which motion do you prioritize?" Whichever drives more of the feature's revenue, but with partners near half of all revenue, leaving them out would be a mistake either way.
  • "How do you keep the message consistent?" One message house, framed differently for an owner who decides alone and a buying group that needs a business case.

Practice twist

Now the feature is free for every customer. What's the PMM's goal for each motion instead, and how do you measure it?
