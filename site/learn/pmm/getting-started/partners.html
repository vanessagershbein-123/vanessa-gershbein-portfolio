Who PMMs work with

PMMLesson 1.47 min

Goal: Name the teams a PMM depends on, and what each one needs from you.

Skills: Interviews & career

Make a list of what a product marketer actually controls and it's short. The product belongs to product management, the pipeline to sales and most channels to other marketing teams. Almost everything you ship depends on someone else saying yes. So the job starts with knowing whose yes you need, and what each of those people needs from you.

You sit at the hub of the wheel

PMMs work between a lot of teams. In PMA's 2025 survey, 88.8% said they work closely with product and 81% with marketing1. Bessemer's guide to building the function adds more spokes: customers, sales, engineering and analysts. It also says PMM lines up with demand generation, brand, field marketing and communications2. Real postings read the same way. Duolingo's posting for a Product Marketing Lead names Product, Brand, Performance marketing, Comms, Business Development and Analytics as partners3.

What each partner needs, and what you need back

Every one of these relationships is a trade. You give each team something it can use, and you need something back:

Partner What they need from you What you need from them
Product (PM, design, engineering) Customer and market insight, positioning, the launch plan Roadmap visibility, early access, honest scope and dates
Sales A pitch that works, battlecards, objection handling, training What's happening in deals: wins, losses, objections
Demand generation and performance Messaging, audiences, offers Channel and campaign results
Brand and creative Positioning and key messages A consistent look and voice
Customer success and support Release notes, onboarding content, FAQs Adoption problems, churn reasons, feature requests
Analytics The questions and the goals Dashboards and experiment reads
Comms, analysts and partners The narrative and launch moments Coverage, outside credibility, reach

The table is a summary built from the sources above and from 2026 job postings. One row comes with a caveat. Analysts come up in Bessemer's guide, and Lauchengco's evangelist job covers press and other outside voices, but analysts rarely show up as a full job of their own in postings.

How to get a yes from people who don't report to you

None of these teams reports to you. You can't assign sales a task or tell a PM what to build, so influence is how anything gets done. Lauchengco's advice is to bring product marketers into product planning and have them talk with their PMs often, because what ships and when inevitably changes4. Beyond that, three habits help:

  • Trade, don't ask. Bring sales a win/loss insight before asking them to use your new deck.
  • Show your evidence. "Six of the last ten lost deals cited setup time" moves a PM more than "customers want it easier."
  • Close the loop. Tell people what happened with their input, or they stop giving it.

Common traps

  • Talking only to the PM. Sales and support often hear about the market's problems before product does, because they talk to customers every day.
  • Shipping enablement nobody asked for. Ask sales what's blocking deals before you build anything.
  • Forgetting customer success. They own the customers who renew, and they know why people leave.

In an interview

Expect cross-functional questions such as walking through a project and your part in it, or describing a time you influenced another leader's decision. Pick stories where you changed someone's mind with evidence. Then say what you gave them in return, because that trade is how PMMs get things done without authority.

Self-check: Your PM won't share the roadmap until it's final, and launch is six weeks out. What do you offer to get in earlier?

Something the PM can use right away: the top objections sales hears, recent win/loss findings, or a draft of how you'd position the feature. Trading insight first gives the PM a reason to bring you in early.

Check your understanding

3 questions. Pick an answer to see if you're right.

  1. Sales tells you your new messaging "doesn't work." What should you do first?

    Show answer

    Ask which deals, personas and parts of the message failed, then check win/loss data. The problem could be the message, the training or the target segment. Find out which before changing anything.

  2. What does a sales team need most from its PMM?

    Show answer

    A pitch that works, battlecards, objection handling and training. Roadmap visibility is what the PMM needs from product, and dashboards come from analytics.

  3. None of the teams a PMM works with report to them. Which habit helps most?

    Show answer

    Trade first: bring something useful, like a win/loss insight, before you ask. Influence without authority runs on trading value, showing evidence and closing the loop.
