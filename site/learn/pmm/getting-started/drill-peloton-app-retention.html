Keep paying users from leaving: Peloton's app

PMMDrill 1.310 minIntermediate

Skills: Data & chartsPositioning & strategy

The prompt

The facts.

  • In the quarter ended September 30, 2026, Peloton had 487,000 paid App subscribers, down from 542,000 a year earlier. Monthly churnThe share of customers, or of revenue, lost in a period. among its equipment-owning subscribers rose to 1.7% from 1.6%. About three in four paid App subscribers, and nearly all equipment subscribers, pay month to month1.
  • The decline isn't new. Paid App subscribers went from 621,000 in fiscal 2024 to 552,000 in fiscal 2025 and 503,000 in fiscal 20261.
  • The Peloton App starts with a 30-day free trial that requires a card. After that, App One covers strength, yoga, meditation, outdoor running and three cardio classes a month, while App+ adds unlimited cardio classes2.

Your task: You're the PMM for the Peloton App. Leadership wants to slow the loss of paid App subscribers without cutting prices.

  1. Where would you look first, and what data would you pull?
  2. Write two short messages: one for people who canceled in the last 90 days, and one for App One members who use their three cardio classes every month.
  3. How would you test the messages before sending them to everyone?

Your earlier answer

Sample answer

1. Find out who leaves, and when. Most paid App subscribers can cancel any month, so every month is a decision. Pull cancellations by how long people had subscribed:

  • If most leave right after the free trial, it's an onboarding problem: they never built a routine.
  • If they leave after several months, it's a value problem: the classes stopped feeling new, or life got in the way.

Each group needs a different fix, so don't write a message until you know which one is bigger.

2. Two messages:

  • Canceled in the last 90 days: "Ten minutes is enough to start again. Here are three short classes to get you back in." The ask is small on purpose, because a lapsed user's first barrier is restarting, not price.
  • App One members who hit the cardio limit: "You've used all three cardio classes this month. App+ gives you unlimited cardio, so your plan doesn't stop in week two." It shows up at the moment the limit bites.

3. Test against a holdout. Send each message to a random slice of its group, keep a similar slice that gets nothing, and compare reactivation or upgrades after 30 days. The holdout tells you whether the message caused the change or the month did.

Why it works

  • It diagnoses before it writes: when people cancel points to either onboarding or value.
  • It uses a real product limit, three cardio classes on App One, as the upgrade moment.
  • It measures against a holdout, so a good month isn't mistaken for a good message.

What interviewers push on

  • "Why not just offer a discount?" It can win people back for a month, but it teaches them to wait for discounts and doesn't fix why they left.
  • "How do you know it worked?" Reactivation and 90-day retention against the holdout, not opens or clicks.

Practice twist

Now the goal is the other end of the funnel: more free-trial users still paying after day 30. What changes in your plan?
