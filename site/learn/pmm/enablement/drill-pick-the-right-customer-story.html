Pick the right customer story

PMMDrill 6.312 minIntermediate

Skills: Writing & storytelling

The prompt

The facts.

  • Stripe processed $1.9 trillion in total payment volume in 2025, up 34%. Co-founder John Collison told Bloomberg that Stripe is a preferred vendor for companies building AI applications and is winning share among large enterprises1.
  • PepsiCo's story: many of the small stores and restaurants supplied by PepsiCo's Away From Home division still paid for deliveries with paper checks handed to drivers. In 2024 the team launched an app with Stripe so customers could order and pay online. Stripe's story says four PepsiCo divisions covering 23 or more brands now use Stripe, local payment methods are supported in more than 20 markets, and the work saved a year of engineering time2.
  • Cloudbeds' story: Cloudbeds sells software that helps hotels run their operations, and Stripe has powered Cloudbeds Payments, its built-in payments product, since 2019. Stripe's story says hotels using Cloudbeds Payments saw 15% revenue growth, and properties that added payment methods saw an average 14.8% revenue boost2.

Your task: You support Stripe's sales team, and three prospects are in play this week. For each one, pick the story you'd send, or say that neither fits and what you'd use instead:

  1. A regional snack distributor whose corner-store customers still pay drivers by check.
  2. A company selling booking software to 3,000 independent yoga studios, which wants studios to take payments inside its product.
  3. A 15-person AI startup about to start charging for subscriptions, worried about failed payments and fraud.

Your earlier answer

Sample answer

  1. Snack distributor: PepsiCo. The match is the situation, not the size: small business customers paying by check, moved to ordering and paying in an app. Lead with the problem they share, then the results.
  2. Yoga software: Cloudbeds. Both are software platforms adding payments for the small businesses they serve. Stripe's reported 15% revenue growth for Cloudbeds' hotels speaks to the question this prospect will ask, whether payments will help its studios grow, though it's Stripe's own figure with no comparison group.
  3. AI startup: neither. PepsiCo is the biggest logo, which makes it tempting, but a 15-person startup won't see itself in a global food company. Since Stripe says it's a preferred vendor for AI companies, a story about a similar early-stage AI company very likely exists, so find it. If there isn't one, offer a reference call with a startup customer that uses subscription billing.

Why matching matters

  • Buyers look for customers like themselves, so the situation beats the size of the logo.
  • A mismatched story can backfire by suggesting you don't understand the buyer.
  • When no story fits, a live reference is usually stronger than a stretched case study.

What interviewers push on

  • "Why not just send all of them?" A pile of stories makes the buyer do the matching. One well-chosen story shows you understood them.
  • "How would you fill the gap for AI startups?" Find a customer like them with a clear result, get permission and write the story, then track whether it helps win similar deals.

Practice twist

The snack distributor's CFO asks whether PepsiCo's results are typical. How do you answer honestly?
