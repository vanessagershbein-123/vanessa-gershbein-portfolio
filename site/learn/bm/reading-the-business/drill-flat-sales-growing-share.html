Flat sales, growing share

BrandDrill 2.110 minBeginner

Skills: Data & chartsBusiness math

The prompt

Hypothetical scenario. The brand and all numbers are made up to show the work.

The facts. You're the associate brand manager on Brightleaf, a frozen breakfast sandwich. Here's the latest 52 weeks of scanner dataStore checkout scans that firms like Circana and NielsenIQ collect from retailers and sell to many brands. against the year before:

Year ago This year Change
Category dollar sales $860M $800M −7%
Brightleaf dollar sales $120M $120M Flat
Brightleaf dollar share 14.0% 15.0% +1.0 point
Brightleaf unit share 13.2% 13.0% −0.2 points
Brightleaf average price per unit $4.95 $5.20 +5%
Category average price per unit $4.68 $4.51 −4%
Brightleaf %ACV distribution 88 88 Flat

Your director is thrilled: "Flat sales in a category down 7%, and we gained a full share point. Great year."

Your task: In three sentences, say what's really going on, what worries you, and the one thing you'd check next.

Your earlier answer

  1. Hint 1 of 3 Start with units: dollar sales ÷ average price, for this year and last year.
  2. Hint 2 of 3 Compare how your price moved with how the category's average price moved. Which way did each go?
  3. Hint 3 of 3 Your director is looking at dollar shareYour dollar sales divided by the whole category's dollar sales.. What does unit shareYour unit sales divided by the whole category's unit sales. say, and what does the gap between the two tell you about price?

The formulas

  • Dollar share = your dollar sales ÷ category dollar sales. Unit share = your units ÷ category units.
  • Units = dollar sales ÷ average price, so if dollars hold flat while price rises 5%, units fall about 5% (1 ÷ 1.05 = 0.952).
  • Price premium = your average price ÷ the category's average price − 1.
  • Why read them together: dollar share = unit share × (your price ÷ the category's average price). Dollar share can rise on price alone while unit share falls.

What's really going on

The dollar share gain is real, but it came from price, not shoppers. Brightleaf's sales held at $120 million while the category shrank, so dollar share rose from about 14.0% to 15.0%. Units tell a different story. Brightleaf raised its price about 5%, so the same dollars bought about 5% fewer sandwiches, and unit share slipped from 13.2% to 13.0%.

The category is getting cheaper while Brightleaf gets pricier. The category's average price fell about 4%, which usually means shoppers are moving to cheaper items, store brandsProducts sold under a retailer's own brand name, like Kirkland Signature at Costco. or deeper deals. Brightleaf's premium over the category average widened from about 6% ($4.95 vs. $4.68) to about 15% ($5.20 vs. $4.51).

A strong answer, in three sentences: "We held dollars and gained a dollar share point, but we sold about 5% fewer sandwiches and lost unit share, so the gain came from our price increase while shoppers moved toward cheaper options. Our price premium over the category has more than doubled, which is a risk if more shoppers keep trading down. Next, I'd check our price gap against the store brand and our top competitor, and whether our base velocityHow fast you sell where you're stocked: sales per point of distribution, per store or per TDP. per store is holding."

Why it matters

  • Always read dollar and unit share together. Dollar share up with unit share down means you're winning on price, not on buyers. That can be a fine strategy, but say it out loud.
  • Read share against the category. Flat sales in a shrinking category is a gain. Flat sales in a growing category is a loss.
  • Watch the price gap. A premium that keeps widening works until it doesn't. The panel will show it first: fewer households buying you.

Practice twist

Next year Brightleaf holds its price at $5.20, the category falls another 5% in dollars, and Brightleaf's sales fall 2%. What's its new dollar share, and is that good news?

Answer: $117.6 million ÷ $760 million ≈ 15.5%, up about half a point. Better than the category, but sales are now falling. Check whether units are falling faster than the category's, which would mean the price gap is starting to cost you shoppers.
