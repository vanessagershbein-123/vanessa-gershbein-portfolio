Write the headline

BrandDrill 2.410 minBeginner

Skills: Writing & storytelling

The prompt

Hypothetical scenario. The brand and all numbers are made up to show the work.

The facts. It's your first monthly business review on Lumen laundry pods. Here's the latest four weeks against the same four weeks last year:

Metric vs. year ago
Lumen dollar sales +2%
Lumen units −3%
Category dollar sales +5%
Lumen dollar share 11.2%, down 0.3 points
%ACV distribution 92, up 1 point
Base (non-promoted) units +8%
Promoted units −29%
Share of units sold on promotion 22%, down from 30%

You also know two things: your team cut promotions this year on purpose to protect margin, and a competitor launched a new pod with heavy promotion two months ago.

Your teammate drafted three possible headlines:

  1. "Lumen sales grew 2% this month."
  2. "Lumen lost 0.3 share points due to a number of factors in a competitive month."
  3. "Lumen's share dipped 0.3 points as we cut promotions while a competitor launched with heavy deals, but our base business grew 8%, so we recommend holding the lower promotion plan."

Your task: Pick the best headline or write a better one. Then write the two supporting lines that follow it, and the one decision you'd ask for.

Your earlier answer

  1. Hint 1 of 3 Find the number your director will ask about first. Is it good news or bad news?
  2. Hint 2 of 3 Separate what you chose to do (cutting promotions) from what happened to you (a competitor's launch). Which explains the share loss?
  3. Hint 3 of 3 A strong headline has four parts: what happened, why, what it means and what you want. Test each draft against all four.

Why #3 wins

  • #1 is true but useless. It hides the share loss and gives leadership nothing to decide.
  • #2 names the problem and explains nothing. "A number of factors" tells your director you don't know yet.
  • #3 does the job of a headline. It says what happened (share down), why (promotions cut while a rival promoted), what it means (the base looks healthy) and what to do (hold the plan). Someone who reads only that sentence still knows the month.

The supporting lines

  1. The share loss is in promoted volume. Promoted units fell 29% as we cut promotions from 30% to 22% of units. Base units grew 8%, though part of that is shoppers who used to buy on deal now paying full price, so the cleaner check is base velocityHow fast you sell where you're stocked: sales per point of distribution, per store or per TDP. in comparable weeks without promotion.
  2. The category is growing faster than we are. It grew 5% to our 2%. Our working hypothesis is that the competitor's heavily promoted launch picked up the deal shoppers we stopped chasing, and we'll confirm it with competitor share by retailer.

The decision to ask for

"Hold the lower promotion plan for two more months, and approve one display event at our two biggest retailers next month to defend share while the competitor's launch is still promoting. I'll own it with our Sales lead, and we'll bring base velocity by retailer to the next review before deciding on a full quarter."

The pattern

Headline: what happened + why + so what + what you want. Then two or three lines of evidence, each tied to a driver. Then one decision with an owner and a date. If a number doesn't support the headline, it belongs in the appendix.

Practice twist

Same month, but base units fell 4% instead of growing 8%. Rewrite the headline.

Answer: something like "Lumen lost 0.3 share points and our base business fell 4%, so the problem isn't just lower promotion; we need to find out why fewer shoppers are buying at full price before we decide on spend." The headline changes because the diagnosis changes.
