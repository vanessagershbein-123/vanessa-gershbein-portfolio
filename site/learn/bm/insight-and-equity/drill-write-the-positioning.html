Write the positioning

BrandDrill 4.215 minIntermediate

Skills: Positioning & strategyBusiness math

The prompt

Hypothetical scenario. The brand, the survey and all numbers are made up to show the work.

The facts. You manage Oatlane, a new oat-milk yogurt about to launch in grocery. In a taste-test survey, yogurt buyers rated how important each benefit is to them, and how well each brand delivers it, each on a 1-to-10 scale:

Benefit Importance Oatlane Dairy Greek leader Almond yogurt rival
Taste 9 7 8 6
Creamy texture 8 9 7 5
Protein 7 6 9 4
Gut health (live cultures) 6 8 6 6
Price 5 5 7 5
Plant-based 4 9 1 9

Your task:

  1. Choose a frame of referenceThe category or set of alternatives a brand is compared with. It decides what counts as a point of difference.: who Oatlane competes against.
  2. Name its points of differenceWhat a brand does better than the alternatives that buyers care about and believe. and points of parityWhat a brand must match to be considered at all in its category., with the numbers behind them.
  3. Write a positioning statement: "For [target] who [need], Oatlane is the [frame] that [point of difference] because [reason to believeThe proof behind a brand's claim, such as an ingredient, a test result or a nutrition label.]."
  4. Write a brand mantraThree to five words that capture what a brand stands for, used inside the company to guide decisions. of three to five words.
  5. Name the biggest risk you'd watch after launch.

Your earlier answer

  1. Hint 1 of 3 Before picking a difference, decide who you're competing against. Changing the competitor set changes every comparison.
  2. Hint 2 of 3 For each benefit, compare Oatlane with the best competitor in your frame, then weight the gap by how much shoppers care.
  3. Hint 3 of 3 A point of parity doesn't have to win. It has to be close enough that shoppers don't rule you out.

The math, in two frames

Weighted advantage = importance × (Oatlane's score − the best competitor's score in your frame).

Benefit Importance All yogurt (vs. the better of the two rivals) Plant-based yogurt (vs. the almond rival)
Taste 9 9 × (7 − 8) = −9 9 × (7 − 6) = +9
Creamy texture 8 8 × (9 − 7) = +16 8 × (9 − 5) = +32
Protein 7 7 × (6 − 9) = −21 7 × (6 − 4) = +14
Gut health 6 6 × (8 − 6) = +12 6 × (8 − 6) = +12
Price 5 5 × (5 − 7) = −10 5 × (5 − 5) = 0
Plant-based 4 4 × (9 − 9) = 0 4 × (9 − 9) = 0
  • Why the formula works: importance tells you how much a benefit sways the choice, and the gap tells you whether you win it. Multiplying them stops a big lead on something nobody cares about from outranking a small lead on something everyone cares about.
  • Reading it: against all yogurt, Oatlane loses on taste, protein and price, which are three of the five things shoppers care most about. Against plant-based yogurt, it wins or ties on everything, and wins biggest on creaminess.
  • The common mistake: picking the benefit where your own score is highest. Plant-based ties for Oatlane's top score (9), but the almond rival matches it and shoppers rank it last, so it's worth 0 as a difference.

A strong answer

  • Frame of reference: plant-based yogurt. It's the frame where Oatlane can win. Start where you're clearly better, then widen the frame once the brand is established.
  • Points of difference: creamy texture (+32) and gut health (+12). Protein scores +14 against the almond rival, but Oatlane's 6 trails the dairy leader's 9, so leading with protein invites a comparison with dairy that it loses. Keep protein as quiet support, not the headline.
  • Points of parity: plant-based and price, where it matches the almond rival, and taste. Taste scores +9 against the almond rival, but Oatlane couldn't hold a taste claim against the dairy leader (7 vs. 8), so treat it as clearing the bar, not as a difference.
  • Positioning statement: "For yogurt lovers who've given up dairy but miss real creaminess, Oatlane is the plant-based yogurt that's as creamy as the real thing, because it's made from whole oats and cultured slowly with live cultures." The ingredient claims are part of the made-up scenario.
  • Brand mantra: "Seriously creamy plant yogurt."
  • Risk to watch: a big dairy brand launching its own oat yogurt. Creaminess is easy to copy if a rival with deeper pockets matches the recipe.

What interviewers push on

  • "Why not compete against dairy? That's where the volume is." In that frame Oatlane is behind on taste, protein and price. Win the smaller frame first, then build a reason for dairy buyers to switch.
  • "Is a 7 on taste really parity?" Parity means close enough not to be ruled out. Against the almond rival's 6 it clears the bar. Against the dairy leader's 8 it might not, which is another reason to pick the plant-based frame.

Practice twist

A year after launch, a big dairy brand launches an oat yogurt rated 8 on taste, 9 on creaminess, 5 on protein, 6 on gut health, 6 on price and 9 on plant-based. Recompute the plant-based frame against the best of the two rivals. What's Oatlane's point of difference now?

Answer: creaminess drops to 8 × (9 − 9) = 0, taste turns negative at 9 × (7 − 8) = −9, and price at 5 × (5 − 6) = −5. Gut health holds at 6 × (8 − 6) = +12, and protein shrinks to 7 × (6 − 5) = +7. Gut health becomes the point of difference, so the positioning has to shift there, and fast.
