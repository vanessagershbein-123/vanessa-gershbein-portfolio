Insight or observation?

BrandDrill 4.112 minIntermediate

Skills: Research & insightWriting & storytelling

The prompt

Hypothetical scenario. The brand, the research and all numbers are made up to show the work.

The facts. You're the assistant brand manager on Morning Fold, a frozen breakfast burrito sold in grocery and mass stores. Sales to families with kids are flat. Your research team sends six statements from a usage and attitude survey, ten in-home interviews and household panelA large sample of households that record what they buy, so you can follow the same buyers over time. data:

  1. 58% of Morning Fold buyers have kids under 12 at home.
  2. Morning Fold's sales fell 3% at mass retailers last quarter, while the category grew 2%.
  3. Parents want breakfast to be quick and healthy.
  4. On school days, parents told us they feel they've failed if their kids eat breakfast in the car. Yet 4 in 10 of their kids' school-day breakfasts happen in the car anyway.
  5. In the interviews, kids ate our burrito with one hand while carrying a backpack in the other.
  6. Panel data shows that households who first bought Morning Fold on promotion rarely bought it again at full price.

Your task:

  1. Label each statement a data pointOne value shown on a chart, such as one month's price., an observation, an insight or a truism.
  2. Pick the statement closest to a real insight and say what makes it one.
  3. Turn statement 5 into an insight in this format: who does what, because of what tension, so the brand should do what.
  4. Say what you'd want to learn next, and which research tool would answer it.

Your earlier answer

  1. Hint 1 of 3 For each statement, ask: does it tell you what people do, or why they do it?
  2. Hint 2 of 3 If a statement would be just as true for every brand in the aisle, it's not an insight.
  3. Hint 3 of 3 Look for a tension: a gap between what people want and what they actually do.

The labels

# Label Why
1 Data point A fact about who buys. Useful for targeting, but it says nothing about why.
2 Data point (about the business, not the shopper) It tells you something is wrong, not what shoppers are thinking.
3 Truism True of every breakfast brand, so it can't set yours apart.
4 Insight It names a tension (parents feel guilty about car breakfasts, but car breakfasts keep happening) that a brand can act on.
5 Observation A behavior you saw, without the why behind it.
6 Observation (from data) A buying pattern. It raises a question about whether promotions bring in the wrong buyers, but doesn't answer it.

What makes statement 4 an insight

  • It explains why, not just what. Parents want to give their kids a proper breakfast, and the school run keeps winning.
  • It holds a tension. What they believe they should do and what they actually do don't match, and the gap carries an emotion: guilt.
  • It points to action. A brand can relieve that guilt instead of just being fast.

The test: observation = what people do. Insight = what people do + the tension behind it + why it matters for the brand. If you can't name the tension, you probably have an observation.

Statement 5, rewritten

"School-day parents want their kids to eat a real breakfast, but the morning ends at the car door, so breakfast happens one-handed on the move, and parents feel bad about it. Morning Fold should be the real breakfast that's made to travel: one-handed, no mess, no guilt."

What that changes: - Product: a wrap that doesn't drip. - Pack: a single-serve sleeve. - Message: "A real breakfast, even in the car."

What to learn next

  • Is the guilt widespread? Add a question to the next usage and attitude survey to size it. Ten interviews can spot a tension; they can't tell you how common it is.
  • Does the product actually travel? Run an in-home product test of the one-handed format against today's burrito.
  • Is promotion bringing in one-time buyers? Use the household panel to compare the repeat rateThe share of buyers who come back to buy again. of buyers who entered on deal with those who paid full price.

What interviewers push on

  • "Isn't that just a finding with feelings added?" No. The feeling is what makes it usable. Guilt suggests a message and a product change. "Kids eat in the car" suggests nothing on its own.
  • "How do you know it's true?" You don't yet. Say how you'd size and test it before betting the brand plan on it.

Practice twist

Your agency proposes the line "Breakfast, made easy." Is it built on the insight? Rewrite it.

Answer: no. "Easy" is the truism from statement 3, and every competitor can say it. A line built on the insight speaks to the guilt, for example: "A real breakfast, even when it's eaten in the car."
