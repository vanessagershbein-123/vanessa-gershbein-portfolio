Costco, Amazon or Instacart? Which channel, which pack

BrandDrill 3.312 minIntermediate

Skills: Positioning & strategyPricing & promotionBusiness math

The prompt

Hypothetical scenario. The brand and all numbers are made up to show the work.

The facts. You manage ClearDay, a 24-hour allergy tablet sold mostly in pharmacies. Three channels want something new:

  • Pharmacy today: a 30-count box at $24.99.
  • A warehouse clubA membership store with a small range of items sold in big packs at low prices, such as Costco or Sam's Club. wants a 120-count pack at $39.99. It sells its own store-brand allergy tablets in the same aisle.
  • Amazon wants a 70-count pack at $44.99, with a subscribe-and-save option.
  • A grocery delivery app wants a 5-count "rescue" pack at $8.99, for shoppers who need relief within the hour.

Your task: Decide which pack goes where and at what price. For each channel, name the shopper's job and the biggest risk. Then say how you'd keep the channels from undercutting each other.

Your earlier answer

  1. Hint 1 of 3 Start with the shopper. In each channel, are they stocking up, refilling on a schedule or fixing an allergy attack right now?
  2. Hint 2 of 3 Work out the price per tablet for every pack. Where is the cheapest tablet, and who could switch to it?
  3. Hint 3 of 3 For each channel, name one risk: a partner who loses sales, a store brandProducts sold under a retailer's own brand name, like Kirkland Signature at Costco. next to you, or a price that resets what shoppers think the brand is worth.

Price per tablet first

Channel Pack Price Per tablet
Pharmacy 30-count $24.99 $0.83
Amazon 70-count $44.99 $0.64
Warehouse club 120-count $39.99 $0.33
Delivery app 5-count $8.99 $1.80

The formula: price per tablet = pack price ÷ tablets. Always compare packs this way. Shoppers do, and so do retail buyers.

Channel by channel

  • Warehouse club: stocking up. The 120-count is a different pack from anything in pharmacy, which helps, but at $0.33 a tablet it's less than half the pharmacy price. Risk: your heaviest pharmacy buyers move to the club, and the club's store brand sits right next to you at an even lower price. Consider a slightly smaller pack or a higher price, so the gap to pharmacy is clear but not dramatic.
  • Amazon: refilling on a schedule. A 70-count with subscribe-and-save suits people who take a tablet every day of allergy season. Risk: subscription discounts quietly push the price per tablet down further, and every other channel's buyer will see it.
  • Delivery app: fixing it right now. A 5-count at $1.80 a tablet is expensive, but this shopper is paying for speed, not quantity. Risk: low, as long as it stays small. It carries a high price per tablet and brings in people having a bad allergy day.
  • Pharmacy: what you protect. The 30-count is your anchor price. Keep it out of deep promotions, and give pharmacy partners something the others don't get, such as a pharmacist-recommended display or first access to a new formula.

How to keep channels from undercutting each other

Retailers set their own shelf prices. You steer through the pack, what you charge each retailer and your suggested price.

  1. Make each channel's pack different, so shoppers can't compare like for like and retailers aren't fighting over the same item.
  2. Build a per-tablet price ladder on purpose: biggest pack cheapest per tablet, smallest most expensive, with steady gaps between them.
  3. Watch the most visible price. Online prices are the easiest for every buyer to check, so changes there travel fastest.

What interviewers push on

  • "Why not just say no to the club?" Because club shoppers may not be your pharmacy shoppers at all. If the club brings in buyers you can't reach otherwise, the pack and price just need to limit switching, not prevent the sale.
  • "How would you know if the club pack is stealing from pharmacy?" Track pharmacy sales in areas near the club's stores against areas without one, and check whether pharmacy buyers in the household panelA large sample of households that record what they buy, so you can follow the same buyers over time. start showing up at the club.

Practice twist

The club offers to take a 90-count pack at $36.99 instead. What's the per-tablet price, and is it better for you?

Answer: $36.99 ÷ 90 ≈ $0.41 a tablet. That's closer to the Amazon price and narrows the gap to pharmacy, so it limits switching. Whether it's better depends on whether club shoppers still see it as a good deal next to the store brand.
