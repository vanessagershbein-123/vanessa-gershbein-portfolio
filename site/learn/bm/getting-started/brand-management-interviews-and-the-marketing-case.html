Brand management interviews and the marketing case

BrandLesson 1.414 min

Goal: Know the steps big CPG companies use to hire, what each round is testing, and how to build a marketing case answer that ends in a recommendation.

Skills: Interviews & careerPositioning & strategy

The biggest interview in brand management lasts all summer. Large CPGConsumer packaged goods: everyday products like food, drinks, cleaning supplies and personal care items. companies rely heavily on summer internships as a pipeline to full-time offers1. At Keurig Dr Pepper, MBA brand interns end the summer with a 25- to 30-minute strategy presentation to senior marketing leaders, followed by questions2.

So you're really competing twice: in the interviews that win you the internship, then over the summer that turns it into an offer. The interviews come first.

The steps

Companies publish their hiring steps, and they look alike:

Company The steps it lists
Clorox Online application, an introductory phone interview, possibly a short online assessment, interviews (virtual panels, one-on-ones or in person), then a decision and offer3
PepsiCo Application review, a recruiter conversation, one or more interviews that ask for real examples using its GREAT5 framework, then a decision and offer4
Keurig Dr Pepper Uses HireVue, an automated interview tool, in its campus recruiting2

P&G's process is application, assessment, interviews and offer, and for most roles the assessment is a hard gate: the results alone decide whether your application moves forward. The Peak Performance Assessment measures your experience and work-related attitudes against P&G's competencies, and some roles add an interactive test of cognitive ability. Fail one and you can try again after 12 months. Interviews are behavioral and most last around 45 to 60 minutes. P&G also expects you to do the assessments and interviews on your own, with no real-time help from other people or from AI5.

P&G's own talent team walks through both assessments in this video, including the games in the interactive one and the retake rules.

What each round tests

Yale's career office says CPG interviews typically combine behavioral and case interviews, reflecting the industry's emphasis on structured thinking and practical business judgment1.

  • Assessments screen at scale. Treat them like an exam: learn the format in advance so nothing surprises you.
  • Behavioral interviews check whether you've already done the job somewhere else: led without authority, delivered a result, recovered from a setback. Bring specific stories with numbers in them.
  • Case interviews check whether you think like the owner of a business. P&G tells MBA candidates that its brand internship "isn't a marketing internship," but a general management one5.

The marketing case isn't a consulting case

A Chicago Booth student who came in ready for behavioral questions was surprised by marketing cases, which were nothing like consulting cases: much shorter and more to the point. At a Booth prep workshop, a Booth alum working in marketing at Kraft Heinz explained that companies love cases because they mirror challenges brand managers face every day, and told students to start with the customer6.

A year later, at another Booth prep session, alumni agreed on one top tip: consider the consumer first, and use a consumer insight as the thread that ties your analysis and recommendation together7.

Formats vary. A Rice MBA student's interviews for a Campbell's brand internship were a phone screen with one associate brand manager, then a final round with two senior associate managers that included a case8.

A structure that ends in a recommendation

There's no official framework, but strong answers tend to move in this order:

  1. Clarify the goal. Growth, share, profit or defense? Over what time? What number would mean success?
  2. Start with the consumer. Who buys, why, and what's changing in what they need or choose.
  3. Look at the company and the competition. Your brand's strengths, costs and capacity; what rivals and store brandsProducts sold under a retailer's own brand name, like Kirkland Signature at Costco. are doing; what the retailer wants.
  4. Choose who and how. With the STP model, you segment the market, select a target and position your product for it9.
  5. Build the plan with the 4Ps: product, price, place and promotion9.
  6. Run the number that decides it. Breakeven volume, cannibalization or payback.
  7. Recommend, with a test. Say what you'd do, what result would change your mind and how you'd test it before going national.

Interviewers listen for a clear recommendation, math you show out loud and a consumer insight that holds the answer together. "It depends" is where a strong answer starts, never where it ends.

Self-check: An interviewer says, "Our frozen pizza brand lost two share points at one retailer this year. What would you do?" Write your first three questions.

Something like: Is the loss only at this retailer, or everywhere? Did anything change on shelf there: price, promotions, displays, distribution or out-of-stocks? Who are we losing shoppers to, a rival brand or the store brand, and why? Those three questions split the problem into the retailer, the shelf and the consumer before you reach for a fix.

Check your understanding

4 questions. Pick an answer to see if you're right.

  1. The night before a P&G online assessment, a friend who works there offers to sit beside you and help. What do you do?

    Show answer

    Thank them and take it alone, since P&G expects no real-time help and the result can decide whether you advance. P&G asks candidates to complete assessments without real-time help from people or AI. Prepare beforehand by learning the format.

  2. The case opens with, "Should we launch a premium line?" What's your first move?

    Show answer

    Clarify the goal and how success will be measured, then start with the consumer. Frameworks come after you know what you're solving for. Booth alumni's top tip was to put the consumer first.

  3. You've laid out a careful structure, and the interviewer asks, "So what would you do?" What makes the strongest answer?

    Show answer

    A clear recommendation, the number behind it and how you'd test it. Cases mirror real brand decisions, and brand managers make the call. End with a recommendation and a test.

  4. For a behavioral question about leading without authority, which story works best?

    Show answer

    A time you got another team to change its plans for your project, with the result in numbers. Brand managers work through Sales, R&D, Operations and agencies, and none of them report to you. Show you've moved people you didn't manage.
