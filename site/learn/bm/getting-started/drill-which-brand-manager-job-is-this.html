Which brand manager job is this?

BrandDrill 1.38 minBeginner

Skills: Interviews & career

The prompt

Hypothetical postings. The companies are made up, and the wording is written to show the patterns you'll see in real ones.

The facts. Three postings, all titled "Brand Manager":

  • A. Harvest Lane Foods, frozen meals. "Own the brand's annual plan and P&LProfit and loss statement: sales at the top, costs below, and profit at the bottom.. Read Circana and shipment data weekly and lead the monthly business review. Partner with Sales on customer plans, with Finance on the forecast and with R&D on product improvements. Manage the A&PAdvertising and sales promotion (or advertising and promotion): the brand's budget for reaching consumers directly. budget and our agencies."
  • B. Fieldnote, project-management software. "Be the guardian of our brand identity across web, product and events. Keep our voice and visual guidelines current, brief design and agency partners, run brand campaigns, and track awareness and social engagement."
  • C. Ridgeback, a fast-growing pet food startup sold online and at a national pet chain. "Run the brand's growth with our founders: manage our Amazon and retail accounts, plan promotions, own our social channels and creator program, and report weekly sales and margin."

Your task: For each posting, say which kind of brand manager job it is and name the two phrases that told you. Then say how you'd prep differently for an interview at A versus B.

Your earlier answer

  1. Hint 1 of 3 Ignore the titles. In each posting, underline what you would own and what you would be judged on.
  2. Hint 2 of 3 Sort the words you underlined: business words (P&L, share, forecast, shipments) versus brand words (identity, voice, guidelines, awareness).
  3. Hint 3 of 3 A posting can be both. For each one, ask which interview question it would most likely open with: a case with numbers, or a walk through your creative work?

The answers

Posting Kind of job Phrases that give it away
A CPG brand management "P&L", "Circana and shipment data", "monthly business review", "Finance on the forecast"
B Brand identity and marketing "guardian of our brand identity", "voice and visual guidelines", "awareness and social engagement"
C A blend of both, common at smaller companies "Amazon and retail accounts" and "weekly sales and margin" (business), plus "social channels and creator program" (brand)

How you'd prep differently

  • For A, expect a marketing case with math: breakeven, a P&L, a promotion that may not pay back, or a business to diagnose from share and distribution data. Bring behavioral stories about getting Sales or Finance to back your plan.
  • For B, expect to walk through campaigns: one you'd run, one you'd critique, and how you'd keep a brand consistent across many teams. A portfolio or work samples may matter more than math.
  • For C, prep both, plus channel economics: what Amazon fees and retailer promotions do to margin, and how you'd split a small budget between selling and storytelling.

Why it matters

The title doesn't tell you what the interview will test. The verbs in the posting do. Match your "why brand management?" answer to the job you're actually interviewing for, too: "I want to run a business" lands at A and falls flat at B.

Practice twist

A posting is titled "Talent Brand Manager." Which kind is it?

Answer: neither. Talent brand (or employer brand) roles market the company to job candidates, usually inside recruiting or HR. One more reason to read past the title.
