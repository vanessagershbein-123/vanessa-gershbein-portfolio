Talk me through this chart

SkillDrill 12 minBeginner

Skills: Interviews & career

The prompt

Hypothetical scenario: Pennywhistle Cold Brew is a made-up brand, and every number here is invented for practice.

You're interviewing for an associate brand manager role at Pennywhistle Cold Brew, which sells bottled cold brew in grocery stores, in club stores and through its own subscription app. The brand director slides a chart across the table and says: "Talk me through it. You've got about a minute."

Start the timer, then say your answer out loud before you type it.

Saved in this browser as you type.

Your earlier answer

  1. Hint 1 of 3 Start with what's measured, in what units, over what period, and where the axisThe ruler along the side or bottom of a chart that says what each position means. starts.
  2. Hint 2 of 3 For the biggest movement, compare each channel's third quarter of 2026 with its third quarter of 2025, not with the quarter before.
  3. Hint 3 of 3 End with what it means for the brand, and one question you'd ask next.

Sample answer

"This is Pennywhistle's net sales by channel, in millions of dollars per quarter, from the start of 2024 through the third quarter of 2026, with the axis starting at zero. In the third quarter of 2026, grocery brought in $10.2 million. The biggest movement is the app: it went from $2.4 million to $3.2 million against the same quarter a year earlier, up a third, while grocery slipped from $10.6 million to $10.2 million, down about 4%, in what should be its strongest season. So the business is moving toward the app and club, and grocery, still the biggest channel, is shrinking at the peak. I'd want to know whether grocery lost distribution or velocity, and how profitable an app customer is."

Show the math

Year-over-year growth = (this period − same period last year) ÷ same period last year.

  • App: ($3.2M − $2.4M) ÷ $2.4M = 0.8 ÷ 2.4 = +33%
  • Grocery: ($10.2M − $10.6M) ÷ $10.6M = −0.4 ÷ 10.6 = −3.8%
  • Club: ($5.2M − $4.6M) ÷ $4.6M = +13%

Why compare with the same quarter a year earlier: grocery is seasonal here. It rises in the second and third quarters and dips in the fourth, every year on the chart. Comparing grocery's third quarter with its second would mix the season into the trend. Comparing quarter with same quarter holds the season constant, and it works for club and the app too, which grow every quarter.

Common mistake: reading grocery's rise from $10.0M in the second quarter to $10.2M in the third as growth. It's the season. Against last year, grocery is down.

Why it works

  • It follows the routine in order and stays under a minute.
  • The one data pointOne value shown on a chart, such as one month's price. is exact, with its unit and period.
  • The biggest movement is sized and compared fairly, quarter against the same quarter.
  • The so-what links the chart to a decision and ends with a sharp next question.

What interviewers push on

  • "Is the app growth good news?" Only if app customers are profitable and stay. Ask about retention and what it costs to win a subscriber.
  • "Why might grocery be down?" Lost shelf space, fewer sales per store, a competitor's promotion, or shoppers moving to the app. Each needs different data.
  • "What chart would you add?" Grocery split into distribution and velocity, or the app's subscriber retention by signup month.

Practice twist

The director adds: "The app's price per bottle is 20% higher than grocery's." Redo your so-what in two sentences.
