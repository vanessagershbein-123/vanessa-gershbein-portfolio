Build the bridge

SkillDrill 410 minIntermediate

Skills: Business math

The prompt

Hypothetical scenario: Pennywhistle Cold Brew is a made-up brand, and every number here is invented for practice.

Pennywhistle's CFO wants one chart that explains how net sales got from last year to this year. You have units sold (millions of bottles) and the average net price per bottle the company received, by channel.

Channel Units last year Net price last year Units this year Net price this year
Grocery 16.0 $2.50 15.2 $2.60
Club 10.0 $1.80 11.0 $1.85
App 3.0 $3.00 4.0 $3.10

Build the bridge from last year's net sales to this year's, with a volume step and a price step for each channel. Then give the CFO a one-sentence headline.

0:00 target 10:00

Saved in this browser as you type.

Your earlier answer

  1. Hint 1 of 3 Start by working out each channel's sales last year and this year: units × price.
  2. Hint 2 of 3 Split each channel's change into two steps: what the change in units did at last year's price, and what the change in price did on this year's units.
  3. Hint 3 of 3 Check that your six steps add up exactly to the total change before you draw the bars.

Sample answer

Last year: grocery 16.0 × $2.50 = $40.0M; club 10.0 × $1.80 = $18.0M; app 3.0 × $3.00 = $9.0M. Total $67.0M.

This year: grocery 15.2 × $2.60 = $39.52M; club 11.0 × $1.85 = $20.35M; app 4.0 × $3.10 = $12.40M. Total $72.27M.

Headline: "Net sales grew $5.27 million, or 7.9%. Extra app volume added the most, $3.0 million; price added $2.47 million across all three channels; and grocery lost $2.0 million of volume that its price increase only partly made up."

Show the math

Volume step = (units this year − units last year) × price last year. Price step = (price this year − price last year) × units this year.

Channel Volume step Price step Total
Grocery (15.2 − 16.0) × $2.50 = −$2.00M ($2.60 − $2.50) × 15.2 = +$1.52M −$0.48M
Club (11.0 − 10.0) × $1.80 = +$1.80M ($1.85 − $1.80) × 11.0 = +$0.55M +$2.35M
App (4.0 − 3.0) × $3.00 = +$3.00M ($3.10 − $3.00) × 4.0 = +$0.40M +$3.40M
All +$2.80M +$2.47M +$5.27M

Why it adds up exactly: sales = price × units. Last year's sales are P₀ × Q₀ and this year's are P₁ × Q₁. The volume step is P₀ × (Q₁ − Q₀) and the price step is (P₁ − P₀) × Q₁. Add them: P₀Q₁ − P₀Q₀ + P₁Q₁ − P₀Q₁ = P₁Q₁ − P₀Q₀, the whole change, with nothing left over.

Growth: $5.27M ÷ $67.0M = 7.9%.

Common mistake: using last year's units in the price step, ($2.60 − $2.50) × 16.0 = $1.60M for grocery. Then the steps miss the small piece where price and volume changed together, (0.10 × −0.8) = −$0.08M, and the bridge doesn't land on this year's total. Pick one convention and say which one you used.

Why it works

  • Every step is a number the CFO can trace back to the table.
  • The chart starts and ends on totals, so the eye reads it as a walk.
  • The headline names the biggest step, its sign, and the warning inside the good news.

What interviewers push on

  • "Is the grocery price increase working?" It added $1.52M but grocery units fell 5%. Ask whether the drop came from shoppers reacting to price or from lost distribution.
  • "What about mix?" Shifting units toward the app, the highest-priced channel, lifts the average price per bottle even if no single price changes. A total-company bridge would show that as a mix step.
  • "Which step would you dig into first?" Grocery volume: it's the only negative step, and grocery is the biggest channel.

Practice twist

Next year the app's price goes to $3.40 and app units grow to 4.5 million, while everything else holds. Add the two app steps to a new bridge and say whether the price increase was worth it if the app would otherwise have grown to 5.0 million units at $3.10.
