Pick the chart for the question

SkillDrill 26 minBeginner

Skills: Writing & storytelling

The prompt

Hypothetical scenario: Pennywhistle Cold Brew is a made-up brand, and every number here is invented for practice.

Pennywhistle's leadership team meets Monday. Each leader has sent you one question, and you have the data for all of them. For each question, name the relationship you're showing, the chart you'd build, and one setup choice that makes it read well.

  1. CEO: "How have weekly app orders trended since we launched the app two years ago?"
  2. Head of sales: "Which of our eight flavors sells the most units, and how far behind is the rest?"
  3. CFO: "How did our sales mix across grocery, club and the app change from last year to this year?"
  4. Head of retail: "Do stores that give us more shelf facings sell more bottles per week?"
  5. Brand director: "How big is each channel in the cold-brew category, and what share do we hold in each?"
  6. Head of the app: "How spread out are subscription order sizes? Is there a typical order?"

Your earlier answer

  1. Hint 1 of 3 For each question, finish the sentence "I want to show ___" before you think about chart types.
  2. Hint 2 of 3 Sort each question into one relationship: change over time, ranking, part-to-whole, correlationHow tightly two measures move together in a straight line, from −1 to 1., distribution, or size and share at once.
  3. Hint 3 of 3 Add one setup detail per chart, such as sorting the bars, highlighting one seriesOne set of values that belong together on a chart, such as one channel's sales by quarter., adding a trend lineThe straight line that best fits the dots in a scatter plot. or starting the axisThe ruler along the side or bottom of a chart that says what each position means. at zero.

Sample answer

# Question Relationship Chart Setup that makes it read
1 App orders since launch Change over time Line chart of weekly orders Mark launches and promotions on the line; if weeks are noisy, add a four-week average
2 Best-selling flavors Ranking Horizontal bars Sort from most to fewest units and print each value at the end of its bar
3 Channel mix, last year vs. this year Part-to-whole over time Two 100% stacked bars, one per year Keep the channel order and colors the same in both bars; put total growth in the title
4 Shelf facings vs. weekly sales Correlation Scatter plot, one dot per store Add a trend line and label the outliers; say plainly that it shows association, not cause
5 Channel size and our share Size and share at once Marimekko Order channels from largest to smallest and highlight Pennywhistle's slice
6 Order sizes Distribution Histogram Use equal-width bins (one bottle each) and mark the median

Why it works

  • Every chart comes from the question's relationship, not from a favorite chart type.
  • Each setup choice removes one thing the reader would otherwise have to work out.
  • Question 4 names the trap before anyone falls in: busy stores may get more facings because they already sell well.

What interviewers push on

  • "Why not a pie for question 3?" Two pies side by side make readers compare angles across circles. Stacked barsA bar split into parts, showing both the total and its pieces. line the parts up.
  • "Could question 5 be two charts?" Yes: channel sizes as sorted bars, and your share by channel as a second bar chart. The MarimekkoA 100% stacked bar chart where each bar's width shows its segment's size, so it shows size and share at once. is one picture, but harder to read with many players.
  • "What if the app has only six months of data?" Then question 1 is a short line, and the honest title says so.

Practice twist

The CEO changes question 1 to: "Are app customers who joined this year sticking around better than last year's?" Which chart now, and how would you set it up?
