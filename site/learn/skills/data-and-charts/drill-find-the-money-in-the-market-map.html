Find the money in the market map

SkillDrill 510 minIntermediate

Skills: Sizing & forecastingPositioning & strategy

The prompt

Hypothetical scenario: Pennywhistle Cold Brew is a made-up brand, and every number here is invented for practice.

Pennywhistle's brand director is planning next year and asks you: "Where's the money for us?" Here's the latest market mapA chart of how a market splits by segment and by player. of US refrigerated cold brew.

Size at least three options, recommend one, and name its biggest risk.

0:00 target 10:00

Saved in this browser as you type.

Your earlier answer

  1. Hint 1 of 3 Read the widths first: which channels hold the most money?
  2. Hint 2 of 3 Then read Pennywhistle's slice in each channel, and notice where it has no slice at all.
  3. Hint 3 of 3 Size every option the same way: the channel's size × the share points you think you could realistically win.

Sample answer

"Grocery is half the category, $600 million of $1.21 billion, and we hold only 8% of it. Every share point there is worth $6 million, more than twice a point in club. Winning two more points in grocery is a $12 million prize. Convenience is the white spaceA large part of a market where a brand is small or absent.: $205 million and we're not in it; a 5% share would be about $10 million, but we'd need cold storage and a way to deliver to small stores. Club is a $260 million channel where private label holds 46%, so four more points, about $10 million, would mean taking share from the store's own brand. I'd go after grocery first: it's the biggest pool, we already have shelf space there, and our grocery sales are slipping, so defending and growing it matters most. The biggest risk is that we buy those points with promotions that cost more than they earn, so I'd test it in a few regions first."

Show the math

Value of one share point = channel size × 1%.

Channel Size One point Pennywhistle today Option
Grocery $600M $6.0M 8% = $48.0M +2 points = $12.0M
Club $260M $2.6M 10% = $26.0M +4 points = $10.4M
Convenience $205M $2.05M 0% = $0 Enter at 5% = $10.25M
Online $145M $1.45M 12% = $17.4M +3 points = $4.35M

Total category: $600M + $260M + $205M + $145M = $1,210M. Grocery is $600M ÷ $1,210M = 50%.

Why it works: a channel's width is its size, and Pennywhistle's height in that column is its share, so sales = width × height. A share point is worth more in a wider column.

Common mistake: comparing heights alone. Pennywhistle's 10% in club looks bigger than its 8% in grocery, but grocery is worth $48 million to the brand and club $26 million.

Note: these are retail sales, what shoppers pay. Pennywhistle's own revenue is smaller after retailer margins (except on its own app, where it is the retailer), and the profit in each option depends on the price and margin in each channel.

Why it works

  • It sizes every option on the same basis, so they can be compared.
  • It names the white space but doesn't pretend entering a new channel is easy.
  • The recommendation ties back to what's happening in the business, and the risk comes with a way to test it.

What interviewers push on

  • "Why not convenience? It's a whole new market." Fair, but it needs cold storage and store-door delivery, and Brand A and Brand B hold 71% of it. A regional test is the cheap way to find out.
  • "What would it cost to win two grocery points?" Shelf space, promotions and maybe a new pack size. Compare that cost with the profit on $12 million of retail sales, not the sales themselves.
  • "Is online a bigger opportunity than it looks?" Pennywhistle's online sales include its own app, its highest-priced channel and one where no retailer takes a cut, so a dollar there may be worth more profit than a dollar in club.

Practice twist

The brand director adds: "Convenience is growing 15% a year; grocery is flat." Re-size the convenience option for two years out, and say whether it changes your recommendation.
