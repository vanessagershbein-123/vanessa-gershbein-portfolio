Cheat sheet: read it, choose it, check it

SkillLesson 83 min

Goal: Keep the whole module on one page: a routine for reading any chart, a chooser for picking one and five red flags to check.

Keep this page open while you practice, or print it.

Read it: the 60-second routine

  1. Units and axesThe ruler along the side or bottom of a chart that says what each position means.. What's measured, in what unit, over what period, from what source. Where does the value axis start?
  2. One data pointOne value shown on a chart, such as one month's price.. One exact value, with its unit and date.
  3. The biggest movement. The largest change, gap or outlierA data point far from the rest., with a rough size: doubled, fell by a third, twice the others.
  4. So what. What it means for the question you were asked, and what you'd check next.

Fill in the blanks: "This is , in , from ___ to ; the axis starts at . In , it was . The biggest change is , about . So , and next I'd check ."

Choose it: start from the question

If the question is... You're showing Start with
How has it changed over time? Change over time Line
Which is biggest? Ranking Bars, sorted
What is it made of? Parts of a whole Stacked or 100% stacked bars
How did we get from A to B? Flow Waterfall
Does one thing move with another? Relationship Scatter plot
How is it spread out? Distribution Histogram with equal bins
How big is each segment, and who leads it? Size and share Marimekko
Just one number? Not a chart Say the number

Check it: five red flags

  1. The axis. Bars must start at zero. A cut axis turns a small gap into a big one.
  2. The window. Does the chart start at a spike or end at a dip? Widen it, and compare a season with the same season a year earlier.
  3. The denominatorThe number you divide by when you turn a total into a rate or an average.. Totals across different sizes need dividing: per store, per person, per visitor or an indexA series rescaled so a chosen starting point equals 100. An index of 120 means 20% above the start..
  4. The definitions. Does every bar, column and player count the same thing?
  5. The title. Does the data back it? Two lines moving together don't prove one drives the other.

When you present a chart

Lead with a takeaway titleA chart or slide title that states the message, not just the topic. that states the message. Highlight the one seriesOne set of values that belong together on a chart, such as one channel's sales by quarter. that proves it, gray the rest, label directly and cut everything else.
