Brand and CPG

TermMeaningUsed in
A&SPAdvertising and sales promotion (or advertising and promotion): the brand's budget for reaching consumers directly. Also written as: A&P.Brand
ABMAssociate brand manager: the usual first brand management role, often hired after an MBA. Also written as: ABMs.Brand
ACVAll commodity volume: a store's total sales of everything it sells. Data firms use it to weight how big each store is. Also written as: all commodity volume.Brand
%ACVPercent ACV distribution: how much of a market's total store sales (its all commodity volume, or ACV) happen in stores that carry your item. At 80% ACV, the stores carrying you do 80% of all sales. Also written as: % ACV, percent ACV, ACV distribution.Brand
base volumeWhat a brand would sell at its regular price, with no promotion running. Also written as: base sales.Brand
breakeven liftHow much sales must rise during a promotion just to earn the same profit as not running it.Brand
buy rateWhat each buying household spends on you in a period. Also written as: buying rate.Brand
CAGRCompound annual growth rate: the steady yearly growth rate that gets you from a starting value to an ending one. Also written as: compound annual growth rate.All paths
category captainA supplier, often the category leader, that a retailer asks to advise on the whole category. Also written as: category captains.Brand
category managementRunning a product category as its own business, deciding assortment, price, promotion and shelf space together.Brand
club storeA membership store with a small range of items sold in big packs at low prices, such as Costco or Sam's Club. Also written as: club stores, warehouse club, warehouse clubs.Brand
co-op advertisingBrand money that helps pay for a retailer's ads featuring the brand. Also written as: cooperative advertising.Brand
contribution marginPrice minus the variable cost of one more unit: what each unit adds toward fixed costs and profit.All paths
CPGConsumer packaged goods: everyday products like food, drinks, cleaning supplies and personal care items. Also written as: consumer packaged goods.All paths
dollar shareYour dollar sales divided by the whole category's dollar sales.Brand
double jeopardyThe pattern that smaller brands have fewer buyers, and those buyers are also slightly less loyal.Brand
drop sizeHow much product a truck leaves at one store on one stop.Brand
DSDDirect store delivery: the brand's own trucks, or distributors working for it, deliver to each store and often stock the shelves, skipping the retailer's warehouse. Also written as: direct store delivery.Brand
DTCDirect to consumer: a brand selling straight to shoppers through its own website, app, subscription or stores. Also written as: direct to consumer, direct-to-consumer.All paths
EBITDAEarnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization: a common measure of operating profit.All paths
elasticityHow much the quantity sold changes when price changes. An elasticity of −1.5 means a 10% price rise cuts units about 15%. Also written as: price elasticity, elasticities.All paths
ERPEnterprise resource planning: the software that runs a company's orders, inventory, shipping and finance.All paths
forward buyingA retailer buying extra during a deal to sell after the deal ends. Also written as: forward buy.Brand
gross marginGross profit as a share of net sales: what's left after the cost of making and shipping the product.All paths
gross salesSales at list price, before trade discounts and returns.Brand
household panelA large sample of households that record what they buy, so you can follow the same buyers over time. Also written as: panel data, consumer panel.Brand
incremental volumeThe extra units a promotion adds on top of base volume.Brand
JBPJoint business plan: a yearly plan a brand and a retailer build together, with shared goals for sales, promotions, shelf and media. Also written as: joint business plan, joint business plans, joint business planning.Brand
key accountOne of a brand's biggest retail customers, usually served by its own dedicated team. Also written as: key accounts.Brand
lappingComparing against the same period a year earlier, especially when last year had something unusual, like a launch.Brand
line reviewA retailer's scheduled review of which items stay, go or get added in a category. Also written as: line reviews.Brand
markupProfit as a share of cost rather than of price. A $1.50 profit on a $3.50 cost is a 43% markup.All paths
net price realizationHow much of a price change actually reached net sales, after discounts and promotions.Brand
net salesSales after discounts, returns and trade promotions: the revenue a company reports.Brand
off-invoiceA trade deal taken off the invoice for every unit a retailer buys during the deal period, whether or not it passes the discount on. Also written as: off-invoice deal, off-invoice deals.Brand
opening price pointThe cheapest option in a category at a given store. Also written as: opening price points.Brand
organic salesSales growth after removing currency moves and businesses bought or sold, so you see what the existing brands did. Also written as: organic net sales, organic growth.Brand
P&LProfit and loss statement: sales at the top, costs below, and profit at the bottom. Also written as: profit and loss.All paths
pantry loadingShoppers stocking up during a deal, which can lower what they buy afterward.Brand
penetrationThe share of households that bought you at least once in a period. Also written as: household penetration.Brand
planogramA map of exactly which products go where on a shelf. Also written as: planograms.Brand
post-promotion dipLower sales after a promotion while shoppers or retailers work through the stock they bought on deal. Also written as: post-promo dip.Brand
price-pack architectureThe ladder of pack sizes and prices a brand offers, designed so each kind of shopper finds a size and price that fits. Also written as: PPA.Brand
price/mixThe part of a sales change that comes from price changes and from shifts toward pricier or cheaper items.Brand
pricing powerThe ability to raise your price and lose fewer buyers than competitors would.All paths
private labelProducts sold under a retailer's own brand name, like Kirkland Signature at Costco. Also written as: store brand, store brands.Brand
purchase frequencyHow many times a buyer bought you in a period.Brand
quick commerceDelivery of everyday items in about an hour or less.All paths
repeat rateThe share of buyers who come back to buy again.Brand
retail mediaAds a retailer sells on its website, app, stores and partner sites, targeted with its shopper data. Also written as: retail media network, retail media networks.All paths
retail takeawayWhat shoppers actually bought at the register, as measured by scanner data. Also called consumption. Also written as: takeaway.Brand
revenue growth managementManaging price, pack sizes, product mix, promotions and trade terms together to grow net revenue. Also written as: RGM.Brand
route to marketHow a product gets from the factory to the shelf, and who sells it to the store. Also written as: routes to market.Brand
scan-downA trade deal the brand pays only on units that scan at the register during the promotion. Also written as: scan-downs, scan-based deal, scan-backs.Brand
scanner dataStore checkout scans that firms like Circana and NielsenIQ collect from retailers and sell to many brands. Also written as: syndicated data.Brand
sell storyA brand's pitch to a retail buyer showing how its product will grow the buyer's category. Also written as: sell stories.Brand
sell-inWhat a brand ships to retailers and distributors. It's what the brand books as sales.Brand
sell-throughWhat shoppers buy from the retailer. Also written as: sell-out.Brand
share of requirementsYour share of a buyer's total spending in the category, a common measure of loyalty.Brand
shrinkflationShrinking a pack while keeping its price, which raises the price per unit.All paths
skimpflationUsing a cheaper recipe or fewer features at the same price.All paths
SKUStock-keeping unit: one specific product, such as one flavor in one size. Also written as: SKUs.All paths
slotting feeA one-time payment a brand makes to get a new product onto a retailer's shelf or into its warehouse. Also written as: slotting fees, slotting allowance, slotting allowances.Brand
subsidizationPromoted units you'd have sold anyway at full price, so the discount bought nothing extra.Brand
TDPsTotal distribution points: %ACV added up across every item in your line. It grows when more stores carry you or when stores carry more of your items. Also written as: TDP, total distribution points.Brand
TPRTemporary price reduction: a short-term cut in the shelf price, usually paid for by the brand. Also written as: TPRs, temporary price reduction, temporary price reductions.Brand
trade spendMoney a brand spends through retailers on price cuts, displays and features. Companies record it as a reduction of sales, not a marketing cost. Also written as: trade promotion, trade promotions.Brand
unit shareYour unit sales divided by the whole category's unit sales.Brand
velocityHow fast you sell where you're stocked: sales per point of distribution, per store or per TDP. Also written as: velocities.Brand
warehouse deliveryThe brand ships to the retailer's warehouses, and the retailer moves the product to its own stores. Also written as: warehouse direct, direct-to-warehouse, DTW.Brand

Planning and forecasting

TermMeaningUsed in
forecast biasA tendency for forecasts to miss in the same direction, such as always too high.All paths
MAPEMean absolute percentage error: the average size of forecast misses, in percent, ignoring whether they were high or low.All paths
S&OPSales and operations planning: the monthly process that combines demand, supply and financial plans into one business plan. Also written as: sales and operations planning.All paths

Product marketing and growth

TermMeaningUsed in
ABMAccount-based marketing: marketing aimed at a named list of target companies, coordinated with sales. Also written as: account-based marketing.PMM
ARRAnnual recurring revenue: the yearly value of subscription contracts. Also written as: annual recurring revenue.PMM
B2BBusiness-to-business: selling to companies rather than to consumers.All paths
B2CBusiness-to-consumer: selling directly to individual people.All paths
battlecardA short internal guide that helps sales handle a specific competitor: where you win, where you lose and how to answer objections. Also written as: battlecards.PMM
CACCustomer acquisition cost: sales and marketing spend divided by the number of new customers won. Also written as: customer acquisition cost.All paths
churnThe share of customers, or of revenue, lost in a period. Also written as: churn rate.PMM
freemiumA pricing model with a free plan and paid upgrades.All paths
GTMGo-to-market: the plan for how a product reaches and wins customers, covering the target, message, pricing, channels and launch. Also written as: go-to-market.PMM
ICPIdeal customer profile: the kind of customer that gets the most value from your product and is most likely to buy. Also written as: ideal customer profile.PMM
LTVLifetime value: the profit or revenue you expect from a customer over the whole relationship. Also written as: lifetime value, customer lifetime value.All paths
MQLMarketing qualified lead: a lead that marketing judges ready for sales follow-up, usually based on fit and engagement. Also written as: marketing qualified lead.PMM
MRRMonthly recurring revenue: the monthly value of subscription contracts. Also written as: monthly recurring revenue.PMM
NPSNet Promoter Score: the share of customers who'd recommend you (9 or 10 out of 10) minus the share who wouldn't (0 to 6). Also written as: Net Promoter Score.All paths
NRRNet revenue retention: what a group of existing customers pays now compared with a year ago, after upgrades, downgrades and cancellations. Above 100% means growth from existing customers outweighs losses. Also written as: net revenue retention, net dollar retention.PMM
PLGProduct-led growth: letting the product itself, through free trials, freemium plans and self-serve sign-up, drive how customers find, adopt and expand. Also written as: product-led growth.PMM
SAMServiceable addressable market: the part of the total market your product and channels can actually reach. Also written as: serviceable addressable market.All paths
SDRSales development rep: the person who qualifies leads and books meetings for account executives. Also written as: sales development rep.PMM
SOMServiceable obtainable market: the share of the reachable market you can realistically win in the near term. Also written as: serviceable obtainable market.All paths
SQLSales qualified lead: a lead that sales has accepted as a real opportunity. Also written as: sales qualified lead.PMM
TAMTotal addressable market: the total revenue available if everyone who could use the product bought it. Also written as: total addressable market.All paths
